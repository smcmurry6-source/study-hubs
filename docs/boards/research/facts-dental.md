## 1. Periodontal and peri-implant classification (2017 World Workshop, AAP/EFP)

**Sources.**
- AAP chart "Staging and Grading Periodontitis" (2 pages), https://www.perio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Staging-and-Grading-Periodontitis.pdf
  (tables reproduced from Tonetti, Greenwell, Kornman, J Periodontol 2018;89 Suppl 1:S159-S172). Cited as "AAP chart".
- Chapple ILC et al. Periodontal health and gingival diseases and conditions on an intact and a reduced periodontium:
  consensus report of workgroup 1. J Clin Periodontol 2018;45(Suppl 20):S68-S77 (opened from a university mirror,
  https://www.umw.edu.pl/sites/default/files/2022-04/Article2.pdf). Cited as "Chapple".
- EFP "Guidance for clinicians" summaries of the workgroup reports: report 2 (periodontitis),
  https://www.efp.org/publications/projects/new-classification/guidance/report-02.pdf ; report 3 (systemic and other
  conditions, mucogingival), .../report-03.pdf ; report 4 (peri-implant), .../report-04.pdf. Cited as "EFP-2/3/4".
- Pilloni A, Rojas MA. Furcation involvement classification: a comprehensive review and a new system proposal.
  Dentistry (Basel) 2018;6(3):34, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6162379/ (Table 1 and text).
- Narrative review of tooth-mobility indices, J Clin Med 2023, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10779763/ (Table 1).

**Periodontal health and gingivitis**
- **PH-1** Gingival health on an intact periodontium: no attachment loss, probing depths of 3 mm or less (no pseudo
  pockets), bleeding on probing (BOP) at fewer than 10% of sites, no radiographic bone loss (Chapple, Table 1, p. S74;
  text p. S71).
- **PH-2** Plaque-induced gingivitis (intact periodontium or reduced periodontium in a non-periodontitis patient):
  BOP at 10% of sites or more with probing depths of 3 mm or less and no periodontitis (Chapple, p. S73; Table 1, p. S74).
- **PH-3** Extent of gingivitis: localized = 10% to 30% of sites bleeding; generalized = more than 30% (Chapple, p. S73).
- **PH-4** Health on a reduced periodontium in a non-periodontitis patient (e.g., recession, after crown lengthening):
  attachment loss present, probing depths 3 mm or less, BOP under 10%, bone loss possible (Chapple, Table 1, p. S74).
- **PH-5** Successfully treated, stable periodontitis patient: health = all probing depths 4 mm or less with **no site
  of 4 mm or more that bleeds**, BOP under 10%; gingivitis in such a patient = BOP at 10% or more at sites of 3 mm or
  less. A 4 mm site that bleeds is no longer a "closed pocket". The patient remains a periodontitis patient for life
  (Chapple, Table 1 and notes, p. S74).
- **PH-6** Table 1 assumes light probing force of 0.2 to 0.25 N (Chapple, Table 1 footnote a, p. S74).

**Periodontitis case definition, staging, grading**
- **PS-1** Three forms of periodontitis are recognized: periodontitis, necrotizing periodontitis, and periodontitis as a
  direct manifestation of systemic disease; the "chronic" vs "aggressive" split was dropped (EFP-2, p. 1).
- **PS-2** A patient is a periodontitis case when interdental CAL is detectable at 2 or more non-adjacent teeth, **or**
  buccal/oral CAL of 3 mm or more with pocketing over 3 mm is found at 2 or more teeth, and the CAL cannot be explained
  by non-periodontal causes (traumatic recession, cervical caries, distal of a second molar from third-molar
  malposition or extraction, an endodontic lesion draining through the marginal periodontium, a vertical root
  fracture) (EFP-2, p. 3).
- **PS-3** Stage is set first by interdental CAL at the site of greatest loss; if CAL is unavailable, use radiographic
  bone loss (RBL). Tooth loss from periodontitis can modify the stage, and one or more complexity factors can shift the
  stage higher (AAP chart, p. 1).
- **PS-4** Severity by stage (AAP chart, p. 1):
  Stage I: CAL 1-2 mm; RBL coronal third (<15%); no tooth loss from periodontitis.
  Stage II: CAL 3-4 mm; RBL coronal third (15%-33%); no tooth loss.
  Stage III: CAL 5 mm or more; RBL to the middle third of the root and beyond; tooth loss of **4 teeth or fewer**.
  Stage IV: CAL 5 mm or more; RBL to the middle third and beyond; tooth loss of **5 teeth or more**.
- **PS-5** Complexity (AAP chart, p. 1):
  Stage I: max probing depth 4 mm or less, mostly horizontal bone loss.
  Stage II: max probing depth 5 mm or less, mostly horizontal bone loss.
  Stage III (in addition to stage II complexity): probing depths 6 mm or more; vertical bone loss 3 mm or more;
  furcation involvement class II or III; moderate ridge defects.
  Stage IV (in addition to stage III complexity): need for complex rehabilitation because of masticatory dysfunction,
  secondary occlusal trauma (tooth mobility degree 2 or more), severe ridge defects, bite collapse/drifting/flaring,
  fewer than 20 remaining teeth (10 opposing pairs).
- **PS-6** Extent (added to the stage): localized = fewer than 30% of teeth involved; generalized; or molar/incisor
  pattern (AAP chart, p. 1).
- **PS-7** Grading estimates rate of progression, response to standard therapy, and possible systemic impact. **Start
  by assuming grade B** and look for evidence to move to A or C (AAP chart, p. 2).
- **PS-8** Direct evidence (radiographic bone loss or CAL over 5 years) is used whenever available: A = no loss over
  5 years; B = less than 2 mm over 5 years; C = 2 mm or more over 5 years (AAP chart, p. 2).
- **PS-9** Indirect evidence, % bone loss divided by age: A = under 0.25; B = 0.25 to 1.0; C = over 1.0. Case
  phenotype: A = heavy biofilm with little destruction; B = destruction in line with biofilm; C = destruction exceeds
  what the biofilm would predict, or patterns suggesting rapid progression or early onset (AAP chart, p. 2).
- **PS-10** Grade modifiers (AAP chart, p. 2): smoking: non-smoker = A, fewer than 10 cigarettes/day = B,
  10 or more/day = C. Diabetes: normoglycemic / no diabetes = A, HbA1c under 7.0% in a patient with diabetes = B,
  HbA1c 7.0% or higher = C.
- **PS-11** "Biologic width" is renamed **supracrestal tissue attachment** (junctional epithelium plus supracrestal
  connective tissue); placing restoration margins within the supracrestal connective-tissue attachment is associated
  with inflammation and loss of support (EFP-3, p. 5).

**Peri-implant conditions** (EFP-4, summarizing Berglundh et al., J Clin Periodontol 2018;45 Suppl 20:S286-S291)
- **PI-1** Peri-implant health: no clinical signs of inflammation, no bleeding/suppuration on gentle probing, no
  increase in probing depth over previous exams, no bone loss (beyond initial remodeling). No range of probing depths
  defines health, and health can exist around implants with reduced bone support (EFP-4, p. 3).
- **PI-2** Peri-implant mucositis: bleeding and/or suppuration on gentle probing with **no bone loss** beyond initial
  remodeling; plaque is the cause, and it can resolve when plaque control is restored (EFP-4, p. 4; Table 1, p. 6).
- **PI-3** Peri-implantitis: bleeding and/or suppuration on gentle probing, increased probing depth compared with
  previous exams, and bone loss beyond initial remodeling (EFP-4, p. 6, Table 1).
- **PI-4** Without previous exam data, peri-implantitis can be diagnosed from bleeding/suppuration on gentle probing
  **plus probing depths of 6 mm or more plus bone levels 3 mm or more apical to the most coronal part of the
  intraosseous portion of the implant** (EFP-4, p. 6).
- **PI-5** Mucositis is assumed to precede peri-implantitis; peri-implantitis progresses faster than periodontitis, in a
  non-linear, accelerating pattern. Strong evidence links a history of severe periodontitis, poor plaque control and
  missed maintenance to peri-implantitis; evidence for smoking and diabetes is inconclusive (EFP-4, p. 5).
- **PI-6** Clinicians should record baseline radiographs and probing after the implant prosthesis is completed;
  epidemiologic bone-loss thresholds should exceed the mean measurement error of 0.5 mm (EFP-4, p. 7).

**Mucogingival conditions and recession** (EFP-3, pp. 3-4, citing Cairo et al. 2011)
- **MG-1** Gingival phenotype (thickness and width of keratinized gingiva) is now part of the classification; recession
  is described by recession depth, gingival thickness, keratinized tissue width, whether the CEJ is detectable
  (class A detectable / B not) and whether a cervical step over 0.5 mm is present (+/-) (EFP-3, p. 3).
- **MG-2** Cairo recession types: RT1 = recession with no interproximal attachment loss (interproximal CEJ not
  visible); RT2 = interproximal attachment loss less than or equal to the buccal attachment loss; RT3 = interproximal
  attachment loss greater than the buccal attachment loss (EFP-3, p. 4).

**Furcation and mobility grading in common use**
- **FM-1** Glickman (1953) furcation grades: I = incipient, suprabony pocket, slight furcal bone loss; II = loss of
  interradicular bone with pocketing but part of the bone/PDL remains (cul-de-sac, not through); III = through-and-
  through; IV = through-and-through with recession so the furcation is clearly visible (Pilloni 2018, Table 1).
- **FM-2** Hamp, Nyman and Lindhe (1975), horizontal attachment loss in the furcation: degree I = under 3 mm;
  degree II = over 3 mm but not the total width; degree III = through-and-through (Pilloni 2018, text section on
  classifications).
- **FM-3** Tarnow and Fletcher vertical subclass (from the fornix): A = 3 mm or less; B = 4-6 mm; C = 7 mm or more
  (Pilloni 2018, text).
- **FM-4** Miller mobility index: 0 = physiologic; 1 = first sign of more-than-normal movement; 2 = up to 1 mm
  buccolingually; 3 = over 1 mm buccolingually and/or depressible (vertical movement). Grades 1-2 are horizontal only;
  grade 3 includes vertical movement (J Clin Med 2023 review, Table 1 and text). Note: the 2017 staging chart uses
  "mobility degree 2 or more" as a stage IV complexity factor (PS-5).
