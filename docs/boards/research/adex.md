## 1. Structure of the exam series

### Big change for our class: the DSE OSCE is being retired; the DLOSCE replaces it

- From **June 1, 2026** the computer-based part of the ADEX dental series is the ADA's **DLOSCE** (Dental Licensure
  Objective Structured Clinical Examination). It is owned by the ADA, overseen by the JCNDE, and used by ADEX under
  an exclusive license. [DLM p.3; DSM p.3 addendum; WEB-T]
- Candidates who registered before 6/1/2026 may still make a first DSE OSCE attempt until **December 31, 2026**; a
  failed DSE must be retaken as the DLOSCE. [DSM p.3]
- **So a class taking ADEX around 2027-2028 takes the DLOSCE, not the DSE OSCE.** The DSE OSCE content outline is
  kept below (section 2b) because it still describes the ADEX view of "diagnosis and treatment planning" and much of
  it overlaps the DLOSCE, but study weights should follow the DLOSCE (section 2a).
- The organization: CDCA-WREB-CITA and ADEX merged into one entity named the **American Board of Dental Examiners**
  (announced 09/17/2025). [DSM p.3]

### Components (2026-2027 season)

| Part | Format | Time | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DLOSCE | Computer, Prometric | ~6 h 45 min seat time (25-min intro/tutorial, timed sections, 3 optional scheduled breaks) | 150 items: 4 sections of 37 questions (75 min each) + a 10-min section with 2 prescription tasks | DLM p.12; CA p.14 |
| Periodontal | Manikin (typodont) | up to 90 min | Calculus detection (4 maxillary teeth), calculus removal (one mandibular quadrant), probing depths (2 mandibular teeth) | CM p.4, 10 |

| Posterior Restorative | Manikin | up to 3.5 h | Class II composite prep + restoration of a carious lesion, mandibular molar or premolar | CM p.4, 20 |

- **All clinical parts are on simulated patients** (manikins with proprietary, magnetically retained typodonts and
  teeth supplied by ADEX); **no live patients**. [CM p.4; CG p.19] Practice versions of the typodonts are sold by
  Acadental. [CG p.19]
- **Periodontal exam becomes required on August 1, 2026.** [CG p.17; WEB-D]
- **Amalgam is discontinued** for the posterior restorative exam (08/01/2026): composite only. [CM p.3, 20]
- Endo: candidates get a radiograph stating the length of #8 at the start; they do not measure it. [CM p.3, 15]
- A typical restorative day: check-in 6:30-8:00 am, anterior 8:00-11:00, grading up to ~30 min, posterior
  11:30 am-3:00 pm (finish time = 3.5 h after the posterior start). [CM p.21]
- Fee (2026): $2,995 for the series (all five clinical parts + DLOSCE) plus facility fee, $150 typodont fee per
  procedure; one free clinical retake per procedure (typodont fee still charged); third attempt $1,495; DLOSCE
  retake $695. [WEB-D]

### Scoring

- **Clinical parts: 75 or more out of 100 passes each part**; results go to candidates and boards as pass/fail
  (failed perio attempts are told their numeric score; failed endo/prosth/restorative attempts get the criteria
  they missed). [CM p.10, 14, 20; CG p.21]
- Three calibrated examiners grade each procedure independently and anonymously; any failing critical deficiency
  or penalty must be confirmed by **two or more examiners** and reviewed by a grading captain. [CM p.10, 13, 20]
- **Rating scale (endo, prosth, restorative)**: ACC = clinically acceptable; SUB = marginally substandard; DEF =
  critically deficient. A criterion rated DEF by two or more examiners gives **zero points for that procedure and a
  failed procedure**. [CM p.14, 20]
- **3-SUB rule**: three independently confirmed SUB ratings on the over-preparation criteria (highlighted yellow
  on the criteria sheets) of one procedure count as critically deficient: the procedure fails. [CM p.14, 20]
- **Periodontal**: 100 points (detection 16, removal 66, complete removal 6, probing 12) minus tissue-damage
  penalties; see section 4. [CM p.10]
- Endo and prosth are each one exam part: failing any one procedure (e.g. the ceramic crown prep) means retaking
  every procedure of that part. Anterior and posterior restorative are separate parts; each part's prep and
  restoration are taken together. [CG p.17; CM p.4]
- Restorative: the candidate restores the tooth even if the prep failed; candidates are not told outcomes during
  the exam. [CM p.20]
- **DLOSCE**: scale scores 49-99, **75 passes**; reported pass/fail; failing candidates receive the scale score and
  performance by area. Single-answer items: 1 point or 0. Multiple-response items: partial credit for some correct
  picks, but **any incorrect pick zeroes the item**; "neutral" options neither add nor subtract. Prescription tasks:
  0-4 points each (strength, quantity dispensed, units per dose, frequency/duration). [DLM p.6-9, p.10; CA p.14]
- Results timing: computer-based and restorative 2-3 business days; perio/endo/prosth 3-4 weeks. [CG p.21]

### Retakes, time limits and fail rules

- **18-month rule**: every part must be passed within 18 months of the first attempt at any part. [CG p.17; CM p.4]
- **10-day wait** after any failed part before retesting. [CG p.17; DLM p.3]
- **3-time fail**: three straight failures of the same part fail the whole series; restart and repay everything.
  After three failed DLOSCEs, wait at least 6 months before a new attempt. [CG p.18; DLM p.3]
- Remediation: ADEX assumes it after a failure but only requires proof if a state board does. [CG p.18]
- **Formats**: **Curriculum Integrated Format (CIF)**, only for current students, over the D3, D4 or final year at
  the student's own school; the 18-month clock starts **July 1 of the year before graduation**, and a CIF part only
  counts as failed after three unsuccessful attempts. **Traditional format**: same content/criteria/scoring, open to
  students, residents and graduates. [CG p.18]
- Eligibility: current students need authorization from a designated school representative; graduates send a
  diploma or transcript. [CG p.6-7]
- Exam-day conduct penalties: see the penalty table in section 4. Misconduct on the computer exam (e.g. sharing
  content) fails the exam and bars re-examination for a year. [DSM p.6]

  dental exam for initial licensure. The map is illustrative; candidates must confirm with the state board. [WEB-M]
- **Alabama** (where UAB students would most often license): the ADEX map does not grey Alabama out, so it is

- CIF is given during the D3/D4 year at the home school [CG p.18], so for a class graduating in 2028 the
  CIF clock would start July 1, 2027. The DECISIONS.md estimate (~Aug 2028) is consistent with taking parts during

### How ADEX differs from the INBDE

| | INBDE (JCNDE) | ADEX series |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | National written board, required by all US boards (knowledge + clinical reasoning) | State clinical licensure exam (hand skills + clinical judgment) |
| Format | 500 multiple-choice items over two days (Day 1: 3 x 100 standalone + 60 case items; Day 2: 2 x 70 case items), 12 h 30 min total [IG PDF p.26] | DLOSCE (150 items, ~6.75 h) + five manikin procedures |
| Item style | One correct answer per item [IG] | DLOSCE adds multiple-response with partial credit and penalty for wrong picks, 3D models, prescription writing [DLM] |
| Pass mark | Scale 49-99, 75 passes [IG PDF p.20] | DLOSCE 75 scale; each clinical part 75/100 [DLM; CM] |
| Content | Foundation knowledge (biomedical, behavioral) integrated with clinical content | Almost entirely clinical: diagnosis, treatment planning, preps, Rx; no basic-science items |

## 2. The computer-based exam

### 2a. DLOSCE (current; what our class will take)

- **Length**: 150 items in 5 sections: four 75-minute sections of 37 questions and one 10-minute section with the
  2 prescription tasks; optional breaks between sections. Not every item is scored (in 2021 the two forms had 129
  and 130 scored items). [CA p.14] ADEX quotes ~6 h 45 min seat time including a 25-min tutorial and three optional
  scheduled breaks; unscheduled break time comes off the clock. [DLM p.12]
- **Content weights** [DLM p.4]:

| Area | Weight | Subtopics |
|---|---|---|
| Restorative | 24% | Diagnosis; preparations; restorations (direct, indirect) |
| Prosthodontics | 19% | Removable; fixed; implants |
| Oral pathology, pain management, TMD | 13% | Oral pathology/oral medicine; orofacial pain and TMD |
| Periodontics | 10% | Diagnosis; treatment planning; etiology |
| Oral surgery | 9% | Diagnosis; treatment planning; extractions |
| Endodontics | 8% | Diagnosis; treatment planning; emergency management; post-treatment evaluation |
| Orthodontics | 6% | Treatment screening; space management |
| Medical emergencies | 6% | Diagnosis; management |
| Prescriptions | 5% | Antibiotic; analgesic |

  Diagnosis, treatment planning and occlusion run across all areas; patients include pediatric, geriatric,
  special-needs and medically complex cases. Universal tooth numbering (1-32, A-T). [DLM p.5]
- **Item styles** [DLM p.5-9; PQ; WEB-T]:
  - **Patient box**: age, sex, chief complaint, history, current findings. Anything missing is unknown or not
    available (e.g. no allergies listed = no known allergies).
  - **Clinical photos, radiographs, and interactive 3D models** of the teeth (rotate, zoom, pan). On the 3D models
    all margins are placed supragingivally. [WEB-T]
  - **Endodontic test tables**: percussion and palpation as +/-, cold as + or "no response", plus EPT values.
  - **Single-response MCQ**, often with long option lists (the published sample has about 14 white-lesion
    diagnoses to choose from).
  - **Multiple-response** ("select one or more"): options are keyed correct, incorrect ("clinical judgment
    error") or neutral; partial credit for some correct picks with no wrong ones; any wrong pick = 0. Some items
    give full credit for any one of several fully correct answers.
  - **Prescription tasks** (2: one antibiotic, one analgesic): choose suitable drugs from a list, then for each
    give tablet strength, number dispensed, number per dose and frequency/duration; 0-4 points each.
- **Style of the practice set, in our words** [PQ]: most stems ask "most likely diagnosis", "what belongs in the
  differential", "which complicates this extraction/implant/RCT", "what must be done before denture fabrication or
  implant placement", which periodontal or peri-implant diagnoses apply, which surfaces have caries or defective
  restorations, and, often, **"which describes this preparation/restoration on tooth N"** shown on a 3D model
  (e.g. a composite, an all-ceramic crown prep, a lithium disilicate prep, a fixed partial denture prep). In the
  70-question set about 10 are single-answer and the rest select-one-or-more. That last stem type is a direct
  bridge to the clinical criteria in section 4: the same eye for over-reduction, margins and contours.

### 2b. DSE OSCE (retiring; kept for reference)

- Computer-based at Prometric; ~4.0 h seat time: 15-min tutorial, optional 15-min break between the two sections,
  **192 min** of test time. Up to **165 items** (varies by form); **150 points** possible, about one-third from
  case-based items; some items worth more than 1 point, some with partial credit; unscored pilot items included.
  **Scaled 75 passes**, reported pass/fail. [DSM p.6, 9]
- Section 1: mostly independent items. Section 2: case-based, with histories, charting, imaging, lab data, models
  and reference material for a patient encounter. [DSM p.9]
- Item types: single-answer MCQ, multiple-response, extended match, drop-down, fill-in-the-blank, hot spot,
  drag-and-drop. [DSM p.9; WEB-CBT]
- Content weights [DSM p.5]: **Patient Evaluation 20%** (pathology 10%, physical evaluation 10%: anatomy,
  systemic conditions, radiology, lab diagnostics, therapeutics); **Comprehensive Treatment Planning 60%**
  (systemic disease/medical emergencies/special care/oral medicine 20%, periodontal diagnosis and treatment
  planning 10%, restorative 18%, specialties 12%: endo, ortho, oral surgery, pediatric, prosth); **Cross-cutting
  clinical judgment 20%** (medical emergencies, infection control).

### 3.1 Restorative (24%) [DSE: Restorative dentistry 18%]

Skills: caries detection on radiographs/photos/3D models, choosing direct vs indirect restorations, judging a
preparation or restoration ("which describes this prep/restoration"), caries prevention adjuncts.

1. ADEX defines exam caries as tissue a sharp explorer enters with light pressure and that shows "tug-back". [CM p.23]
2. A Class II composite box should break the proximal contact but stay within about 1 mm of the adjacent tooth at the height of contour (over 2 mm = critical). [RP1]
3. The gingival floor must be visibly out of contact, again by no more than about 1 mm; a gingival contact that is not visibly open is critical. [RP1]
4. Composite isthmus: 1-2 mm wide and no more than a third of the intercuspal distance; over half is critical. [RP1]
5. Pulpal floor 1-3 mm deep for a Class II composite; deeper than 4 mm or shallower than 1 mm is critical. [RP1]
6. Axial wall depth limits: molars about 2 mm, premolars about 1.5 mm; an axial wall left entirely in enamel is critical. [RP1]
7. A remaining marginal ridge of 1 mm or less (or undermined) is critical over-extension. [RP1]
8. Class II proximal walls: parallel or converging occlusally; margins at 90 degrees; flat gingival floor perpendicular to the long axis. [RP1]
9. Class III on a maxillary central: axial depth no more than 2 mm; on a lateral, no more than 1 mm (thinner tooth). [RA1]
10. Class III outline: keep the incisal angle intact; a facial extension more than 2 mm past the contact or an outline over 5 mm tall is critical. [RA1]
11. Small unsupported enamel may be kept on an anterior only to preserve facial esthetics; posterior margins must sit on sound, supported tooth. [RA1; RP1]
12. Explorer-penetrable decalcification left on a margin is critical. [RA1; RP1]
13. Finished restoration: deficiency up to 0.5 mm or excess up to 1 mm is marginal; beyond that, or an open margin, is critical. [RA2; RP2]
14. An open, concave or irregular contact (food trap), or one floss cannot pass, is critical. [RA2; RP2]
15. A restoration that is the only occlusal contact in its quadrant is critical (grossly high). [RA2; RP2]
16. Gross enameloplasty of neighboring tooth structure is critical. [RA2; RP2]
17. Crown preps graded on DLOSCE-style 3D models: margins on the models are always supragingival. [WEB-T]
18. Fluoride varnish is 5% NaF (2.26% F, 22,600 ppm F); APF gel is 1.23% F (12,300 ppm F). [AAPD-F]
19. AAPD recommends professional fluoride (5% NaF varnish or 1.23% F gel) at least twice a year for caries prevention in at-risk children. [AAPD-F]
20. 38% silver diamine fluoride (5% F, 44,800 ppm F) is FDA-cleared as a desensitizer and used off-label to arrest cavitated lesions; AAPD supports it within a full caries-management plan. [AAPD-F]
21. Toothpaste amounts: a smear/rice grain (about 0.1 mg F) under age 3; pea-size (about 0.25 mg F) from 3 to 6. [AAPD-F]
22. Community water fluoridation target: 0.7 ppm F. [AAPD-F]
23. Dietary fluoride supplements (only when water is low in fluoride): none before 6 months; at under 0.3 ppm water F, 0.25 mg/day from 6 months to 3 years, 0.50 mg at 3-6 years, 1.00 mg at 6 to at least 16 years. [AAPD-F Table]

### 3.2 Prosthodontics (19%) [DSE: Specialties, prosthodontic]

Skills: removable (partial and complete), fixed, implants; reading 3D models of preps and restorations; what must be
done before a prosthesis; what complicates the plan. (Practice items ask these directly. [PQ])

1. Full-coverage crown prep margins on the ADEX exam: between 0.5 mm subgingival and 1.5 mm supragingival. [PC; PZP; PZM]
2. Margin width 0.5-1.5 mm for both all-ceramic and zirconia preps; over 2 mm or under 0.5 mm is critical. [PC; PZM]
3. Beveled margins and cupped/"J" margins are critical errors on all-ceramic and zirconia preps. [PC; PZM]
4. Axial reduction 1-2 mm; over 2.5 mm or under 1 mm is critical. [PC; PZM]
5. Taper: up to 12 degrees per wall acceptable; 12-16 marginal; over 16 critical. [PC; PZM; CM p.16]
6. Incisal reduction on an anterior all-ceramic prep: 1-3 mm (over 3.5 or under 1 critical). [PC]
7. Occlusal reduction on a zirconia posterior prep: 1-2 mm (over 2.5 or under 1 critical). [PZM; PZP]
8. Path of insertion within 20 degrees of the long axis; 30 degrees or more is critical. [PC; PZM]
9. An undercut deeper than 0.5 mm, or one that would ruin margin width once blocked out, is critical. [PC; PZM]
10. Internal line angles must show rounding; excessively sharp angles are critical. [PC; PZM]
11. Anterior all-ceramic prep: lingual wall at least 1 mm high. [PC]
12. Bridge abutments must share a path of insertion; needing more reduction in the apical two-thirds to seat is critical. [PZM; CM p.16]
13. Reduction guides: putty matrices sectioned faciolingually and mesiodistally let you measure reduction. [CM p.16]
14. Implant, removable and occlusion facts: to be sourced in a later pass (no guideline opened yet). UNVERIFIED.

### 3.3 Oral pathology, pain and TMD (13%) [DSE: Pathology 10%; oral medicine]

Skills: white, red, pigmented, ulcerative and bony lesions from photos/radiographs; differential diagnosis lists;
when to biopsy; orofacial pain and TMD diagnosis; analgesic choice.

1. Approach to a white lesion: congenital or acquired, does it wipe off, does it have a pattern (papular, reticular, annular), then everything else. [WL]
2. Leukoedema: a normal variant, usually bilateral buccal mucosa, that fades when the mucosa is stretched. [WL]
3. Pseudomembranous candidiasis wipes off and leaves a red, sometimes bleeding base. [WL]
4. Frictional keratosis is rough and matches an identifiable source of rubbing; remove the cause and re-check. [WL]
5. Reticular oral lichen planus: white striae, often bilateral posterior buccal mucosa; affects about 0.1-2.2% of people, mean age about 55, more often women. [WL]
6. Oral hairy leukoplakia: EBV-related, in immunosuppressed patients, as non-scrapable, velvety white plaques on the lateral borders of the tongue (one or both sides; rarely spreading over the whole dorsum); a marker of progression toward AIDS in HIV. [WL]
7. White sponge nevus: inherited (autosomal dominant), present from early life. [WL]
8. Proliferative verrucous leukoplakia is a distinct, high-risk form of leukoplakia (WHO). [WL]
9. Pooled malignant transformation of oral leukoplakia: about 6.6% (55 studies). [OL-MA]
10. Higher-risk leukoplakia: non-homogeneous (RR about 4.2), larger size, lateral border of tongue (about 12.7% transformed), smokers, and epithelial dysplasia (RR about 2.8). [OL-MA]
11. DLOSCE white-lesion items present long option lists (linea alba through verrucous carcinoma), so learn the distinguishing feature of each. [DLM p.6, described]
12. DC/TMD myalgia exam: 1 kg palpation for 2 seconds; 5 seconds to look for spreading or referred pain. [DCTMD]
13. Disc displacement without reduction "with limited opening": assisted opening (including incisal overlap) under 40 mm; 40 mm or more = "without limited opening". [DCTMD]
14. Joint-noise history can be satisfied by a report of clicking or crepitus in the past 30 days. [DCTMD]
15. Clinical criteria for DD with reduction, DD without reduction without limited opening, and degenerative joint disease have low sensitivity but good-to-excellent specificity; DD without reduction with limited opening has acceptable sensitivity and specificity from the exam alone. [DCTMD]
16. Acute dental pain: NSAIDs alone or with acetaminophen are first line; opioids only when those fail. [ADA-PAIN]
17. Typical first-line doses named: ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg, optionally with acetaminophen 500 mg. [ADA-PAIN Box 1]
18. Daily maximums: ibuprofen 2,400 mg, naproxen sodium 1,100 mg, acetaminophen 4,000 mg (count acetaminophen in combination products). [ADA-PAIN]
19. No opioids after a simple extraction; after surgical extraction only as a rescue, lowest dose, fewest tablets, rarely over 3 days. [ADA-PAIN Box 1]
20. If NSAIDs are contraindicated: acetaminophen 1,000 mg alone. [ADA-PAIN Box 1]
21. Panel suggests against adding corticosteroids for post-extraction pain. [ADA-PAIN Box 1]
22. Long-acting local anesthetic before discharge (0.5% bupivacaine with 1:200,000 epinephrine, or 4% articaine infiltration) is suggested over lidocaine or mepivacaine for post-extraction pain. [ADA-PAIN Box 1]
23. FDA warns against codeine and tramadol in 12-18-year-olds who are obese or have sleep apnea/severe lung disease, and during breastfeeding. [ADA-PAIN]

### 3.4 Periodontics (10%) [DSE: Periodontal diagnosis and treatment planning 10%]

Skills: staging/grading from charting and radiographs, etiology (biofilm, calculus, risk factors), treatment
planning; on the clinical exam, calculus detection/removal and probing.

1. Stage is set first by interdental CAL at the worst site; use radiographic bone loss if CAL is not available. [AAP-SG]
2. Stage I: CAL 1-2 mm, RBL in the coronal third under 15%, probing depth up to 4 mm, mostly horizontal loss. [AAP-SG]
3. Stage II: CAL 3-4 mm, RBL 15-33%, probing depth up to 5 mm, mostly horizontal loss. [AAP-SG]
4. Stage III: CAL 5 mm or more, bone loss to the middle third or beyond, tooth loss from periodontitis of 4 or fewer teeth; complexity such as PD 6 mm or more, vertical loss 3 mm or more, Class II/III furcation, moderate ridge defects. [AAP-SG]
5. Stage IV: as Stage III but 5 or more teeth lost to periodontitis, or needs complex rehabilitation (mobility degree 2 or more from secondary trauma, severe ridge defects, bite collapse/drifting/flaring, fewer than 20 teeth / 10 opposing pairs). [AAP-SG]
6. Complexity factors can raise the stage. [AAP-SG]
7. Extent: localized if under 30% of teeth; otherwise generalized, or a molar/incisor pattern. [AAP-SG]
8. Grade: start at B, then look for evidence of A or C. [AAP-SG]
9. Direct evidence: no loss over 5 years = A; under 2 mm = B; 2 mm or more = C. [AAP-SG]
10. Indirect evidence: % bone loss divided by age: under 0.25 = A; 0.25-1.0 = B; over 1.0 = C. [AAP-SG]
11. Grade modifiers: smoking under 10 cigarettes/day = B, 10 or more = C; HbA1c under 7.0% in a diabetic = B, 7.0% or more = C. [AAP-SG]
12. ADEX probing is graded within +/-1 mm at six sites per tooth (DF, F, MF, DL, L, ML). [PR]
13. ADEX calculus detection is scored on M, D, F and L of four maxillary teeth. [PR]
14. Soft-tissue laceration over 3 mm, papilla amputation, or an unreported broken instrument tip is major tissue damage (automatic fail). [PR]
15. Periodontal scalers are critical items: always heat-sterilize. [CDC16]
16. Gingivitis/peri-implant case definitions from the 2017 workshop: to source (Wiley consensus papers blocked). UNVERIFIED.

### 3.5 Oral surgery (9%) [DSE: Specialties, oral surgery]

Skills: diagnosis, treatment planning and extraction difficulty ("what complicates extraction of tooth N" on a
radiograph), medically complex patients, post-op care.

1. MRONJ case definition needs all three: current or past antiresorptive therapy (alone or with immune modulators or antiangiogenics); exposed bone, or bone probeable through a fistula, for more than 8 weeks; and no jaw radiation or metastatic disease to the jaws. [MRONJ p.~3]
2. Stage 0 (non-exposed variant): no clinical necrotic bone but nonspecific symptoms (odontalgia without a dental cause, dull jaw pain radiating to the TMJ, sinus pain, altered sensation) or clinical/radiographic findings; up to 50% progress to Stage 1. [MRONJ]
3. Stage 1: exposed/probeable necrotic bone, no symptoms, no infection. [MRONJ]
4. Stage 2: exposed/probeable bone with infection or inflammation; symptomatic. [MRONJ]
5. Stage 3: as Stage 2 plus one or more of: necrosis beyond the alveolar bone (inferior border or ramus; maxillary sinus or zygoma), pathologic fracture, extraoral fistula, oral-antral/oral-nasal communication, osteolysis to the inferior border or sinus floor. [MRONJ]
6. MRONJ risk with osteoporosis dosing: about 0.02-0.05% with bisphosphonates; IV zoledronate (osteoporosis dosing) no more than about 0.02%; oral bisphosphonates no more than about 0.05%. [MRONJ p.7]
7. Cancer patients on zoledronate: cumulative risk mostly under 5% (range 0-18%); denosumab similar (0-6.9%). [MRONJ p.7]
8. The 2022 update did not endorse a routine drug holiday before surgery to prevent MRONJ (evidence insufficient). [MRONJ]
9. Oral health maintenance and dental prophylaxis before antiresorptive therapy starts can reduce MRONJ. [MRONJ]
10. Max local anesthetic, lidocaine with epinephrine: keep under 500 mg in healthy adults and never above 7 mg/kg. [LIDO]
11. Articaine with epinephrine: max 7 mg/kg; labeled for patients 4 years and older. [ART]
12. Mepivacaine: total dose at one sitting no more than 400 mg in adults. [MEPI]
13. Use sterile saline or sterile water (not unit water) as coolant/irrigant for surgical procedures. [CDC16 p.15]
14. Surgical instruments are critical items: heat sterilization every time. [CDC16]
15. Third-molar, anticoagulant and dry-socket facts: to be sourced. UNVERIFIED.

### 3.6 Endodontics (8%) [DSE: Specialties, endodontic]

Skills: pulpal and periapical diagnosis from symptoms and test tables (percussion, palpation, cold, EPT), treatment
planning, emergency care, judging completed RCT on radiographs.

1. Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis: spontaneous pain or lingering pain to temperature; vital, inflamed pulp that cannot heal. [ADA-ABX Table 1]
2. Symptomatic apical periodontitis: pain on biting, percussion or palpation, with or without a radiographic lesion, without swelling. [ADA-ABX Table 1]
3. Localized acute apical abscess: necrotic pulp, pus, localized swelling, no fascial-space or lymph-node spread, no fever or malaise. [ADA-ABX Table 1]
4. Acute apical abscess with systemic involvement: adds fascial space or lymph-node involvement, fever and/or malaise. [ADA-ABX Table 1]
5. No antibiotics for irreversible pulpitis (with or without apical periodontitis); treat definitively. [ADA-ABX Rec 1]
6. No antibiotics for necrosis with symptomatic apical periodontitis or localized abscess when definitive treatment (RCT, I&D, extraction) is done; antibiotics as an adjunct do not help. [ADA-ABX Rec 2A, 3]
7. Antibiotics are for abscess with systemic involvement (with urgent definitive care), or a localized abscess when definitive care is not available. [ADA-ABX]
8. DLOSCE endo tables: percussion/palpation as +/-; cold as + or "no response"; EPT values. [DLM p.6]
9. ADEX anterior access: lingual, over the chamber, straight-line access; at least 2 mm from the incisal edge and 3 mm from the lingual CEJ; no wider than half the lingual surface. [EA]
10. Obturation: at the apex to 2 mm short is acceptable; more than 0.5 mm long is critical. [EA]
11. Apical third dense, no voids; no material other than gutta-percha in the canal. [EA]
12. Coronal fill: gutta-percha no more than 3 mm below the CEJ; no more than 2 mm of gutta-percha/sealer above the CEJ; no restorative material in the chamber. [EA]
13. A maxillary first molar (#14) access must reach the tooth's three canals (on a typical #14: MB, DB, palatal); access in the mesial triangular pit/central fossa, at least 2 mm from the mesial marginal ridge; at least 2.5 mm across both ways; whole chamber roof removed. [EP]
14. Perforation, fracture or reducing the crown during endo = automatic failure. [EA; EP]
15. A separated file is acceptable only if it does not affect obturation. [EA]

### 3.7 Orthodontics (6%) [DSE: Specialties, orthodontics and pediatric]

Skills: screening and referral, space management (space maintainers), crossbites, habits, eruption problems.

1. Space maintainer types: fixed unilateral (band and loop, crown and loop, distal shoe), fixed bilateral (lower lingual holding arch, Nance, transpalatal arch), removable (partial denture, Hawley-type). [AAPD-DD]
2. Decide on space maintenance by: which tooth was lost, time since loss, occlusion and space analysis, dental age, presence and root development of the successor, bone over the successor, health history, cooperation, habits and hygiene. [AAPD-DD]
3. Most space maintainers (except crown and loop) had mean survival under 2 years; loss of cement is the usual failure. [AAPD-DD]
4. Keep the appliance until the permanent tooth erupts into position; check integrity and cement at recalls. [AAPD-DD]
5. Late mixed dentition: holding leeway space and treating skeletal disharmony and crowding may start; early placement of a passive lingual arch can improve long-term lower incisor stability. [AAPD-DD]
6. Non-nutritive sucking habits (pacifier, thumb) are linked to anterior open bite and posterior crossbite; early visits should guide parents to help children stop by age 36 months or younger. [AAPD-DD]
7. A simple anterior crossbite is dental (Class I molars, tipped incisors); distinguish it from skeletal Class III. [AAPD-DD]
8. A unilateral posterior crossbite is most often a bilateral constriction with a functional mandibular shift. [AAPD-DD]
9. Ankylosed teeth sink below the occlusal plane (infraocclusion) as neighbors erupt; no physiologic mobility and a dull tone when tapped with a mirror handle point to ankylosis. [AAPD-DD]
10. Dentition stages used in planning: primary, mixed, adolescent (permanent), adult. [AAPD-DD]
11. Tooth notation on the DLOSCE: Universal system, permanent 1-32, primary A-T. [DLM p.5]

### 3.8 Medical emergencies (6%) [DSE: Cross-cutting medical emergencies; systemic disease]

Skills: recognize and manage office emergencies; drug doses for the emergency kit.

1. Anaphylaxis: epinephrine into the anterolateral thigh (IM or SC, through clothing if needed); auto-injector 0.3 mg for 30 kg or more, 0.15 mg for 15-30 kg. [EPI]
2. Severe persistent anaphylaxis may need a repeat epinephrine dose; more than two doses should be given by health care providers. [EPI]
3. Angina: nitroglycerin 0.4 mg sublingual; one more tablet every 5 minutes; if pain persists after 3 tablets in 15 minutes, get emergency care. Onset 1-3 min, peak by 5 min. [NTG]
4. The status epilepticus guideline covers seizures lasting longer than 5 minutes; IM midazolam is more effective than IV lorazepam in adults without IV access (Level A); benzodiazepines are first line. [AES]
5. Penicillin allergy labels: about 90% of labeled patients test negative on skin testing. [AHA21 p.e972] About 10% self-report allergy but under 1% are truly allergic. [ADA-ABX]
6. Fatal anaphylaxis after one cephalosporin dose in someone without a prior serious reaction: estimated under 1 per million doses. [AHA21]
7. Azithromycin: caution with a prolonged QTc (over 450 ms). [AHA21]
8. Hypoglycemia treatment (glucose dose and thresholds), syncope positioning, asthma (albuterol), aspirin for suspected MI, and CPR ratios: to be sourced (ADA Standards of Care and AHA CPR pages were blocked). UNVERIFIED.

### 3.9 Prescriptions (5%) [DSE: Therapeutics]

The DLOSCE has two prescription tasks (one antibiotic, one analgesic); each wants strength, quantity, units per
dose and frequency/duration. [DLM p.9] Self-check: always write all four.

1. Endodontic infection, first line: amoxicillin 500 mg three times a day for 3-7 days, or penicillin VK 500 mg four times a day for 3-7 days (amoxicillin preferred). [ADA-ABX]
2. Penicillin allergy without anaphylaxis, angioedema or hives: cephalexin 500 mg four times a day, 3-7 days. [ADA-ABX]
3. Penicillin allergy with anaphylaxis, angioedema or hives: azithromycin 500 mg on day 1 then 250 mg for 4 more days; or clindamycin 300 mg four times a day, 3-7 days. [ADA-ABX]
4. Not improving on amoxicillin/penicillin: add metronidazole 500 mg three times a day for 7 days, or switch to amoxicillin/clavulanate 500/125 mg three times a day for 7 days. [ADA-ABX]
5. Re-evaluate within 3 days; stop antibiotics 24 hours after symptoms fully resolve. [ADA-ABX]
6. Clindamycin carries a high C. difficile risk even after a single dose; azithromycin has higher resistance rates. [ADA-ABX]
7. Endocarditis prophylaxis only for the highest-risk patients: prosthetic cardiac valve (surgical or transcatheter) or valve repair with devices (annuloplasty, rings, clips); left ventricular assist devices or implantable heart; previous, relapsed or recurrent IE; certain congenital heart disease (unrepaired cyanotic CHD including palliative shunts and conduits; completely repaired CHD with prosthetic material or device during the first 6 months after the procedure; repaired CHD with residual defects at or next to a prosthetic patch or device; surgical or transcatheter pulmonary valve or conduit); cardiac transplant recipients with valvulopathy. Not suggested for pacemakers/implanted electronic devices, closed septal-defect devices, vascular grafts, stents, CNS shunts or vena cava filters. [AHA21 Table 3]
8. Procedures that need it (for those patients): any manipulation of gingival tissue or the periapical region, or perforation of the mucosa. Not needed for injections through non-infected tissue, radiographs, placing or adjusting removable/orthodontic appliances, bracket placement, shedding primary teeth, or lip/mucosal trauma bleeding. [AHA21 Table 4]
9. Prophylaxis regimen, single dose 30-60 minutes before: amoxicillin 2 g (children 50 mg/kg). [AHA21 Table 5]
10. Penicillin-allergic, oral: cephalexin 2 g (50 mg/kg); or azithromycin/clarithromycin 500 mg (15 mg/kg); or doxycycline 100 mg (under 45 kg: 2.2 mg/kg). [AHA21 Table 5]
11. Cannot take oral: ampicillin 2 g IM/IV, or cefazolin/ceftriaxone 1 g IM/IV (children 50 mg/kg). [AHA21 Table 5]
12. **Clindamycin is no longer recommended for IE prophylaxis.** Do not give cephalosporins to anyone with anaphylaxis, angioedema or urticaria to penicillin. [AHA21 Table 5 notes]
13. If the dose was missed, it can be given up to 2 hours after the procedure; if the patient is already on a short antibiotic course, choose a different class (or wait 10 days). [AHA21 p.e973]
14. Analgesic task: NSAID first (ibuprofen 400 mg or naproxen sodium 440 mg), with acetaminophen 500 mg if needed; respect daily maximums (2,400 / 1,100 / 4,000 mg). [ADA-PAIN]
15. Prosthetic joint prophylaxis (ADA 2015 says generally not indicated): ada.org blocked, not re-verified. UNVERIFIED.

### 3.10 Infection control [DSE: Cross-cutting 20%; not a DLOSCE area, but enforced on the ADEX clinical floor]

1. Critical items (surgical instruments, periodontal scalers) penetrate soft tissue or bone: always heat-sterilize. [CDC16]
2. Semicritical items (mirrors, condensers, reusable impression trays) touch mucosa or non-intact skin: heat-sterilize when heat-tolerant (most are). [CDC16]
3. Noncritical items (radiograph cone, BP cuff, facebow) touch intact skin: clean, then disinfect with an EPA-registered hospital disinfectant, or use barriers. [CDC16]
4. Spore tests (biological indicators) at least weekly; chemical indicators inside every package. [CDC16]
5. Dental unit water for routine care must meet drinking-water standards: no more than 500 CFU/mL of heterotrophic bacteria; bottle systems alone are not enough. [CDC16 p.15]
6. Use sterile water or saline for surgical irrigation. [CDC16 p.15]
7. Do not recap needles two-handed; use a one-handed scoop or a recapping device. [CDC16]
8. Use single-dose vials for parenteral medications whenever possible. [CDC16]
9. Hepatitis B vaccine is offered at no cost to at-risk staff; check anti-HBs 1-2 months after the 3-dose series. [CDC16 checklist]
10. On the ADEX clinical exam, a dropped or contaminated instrument must be reported to the Chief Examiner; continuing to use it without that discussion is a 100-point penalty. [CM p.12]

## 4. Clinical sections: grading criteria as self-check lists

How to read these lists: **ACC** = meets the criterion; **SUB** = marginally substandard (costs points; three SUBs on
the yellow "over-preparation" criteria of one procedure = failure, marked **[3-SUB]** below); **DEF** = critically
deficient (if two examiners agree, the whole procedure scores zero and fails). **Critical errors** listed at the
top of each sheet fail the procedure outright. All measurements are the sheet's own thresholds for the 2026-2027
season, paraphrased. These lists are study aids, not the official sheets: always check the current sheet.

### 4.0 Penalties that apply to every clinical part [CM p.28]

Point deductions taken from the procedure's score:

- **-100 (automatic failure)**: not completing an assigned procedure; breaking exam standards, rules or the time
  schedule; treating a tooth or surface that was not assigned/approved; gross damage to adjacent teeth or tissue;
  unrecognized pulp exposure; calling a pulp exposure when there is none; a large exposure that would need RCT; an
  unjustified exposure; an unrestorable iatrogenic fracture; critical lack of clinical judgment or diagnostic
  skill; unprofessional or uncooperative behavior.
- **-21**: asking to remove caries or decalcification without clinical justification.
- **-11 each**: poor simulated-patient management; first prep not yet at acceptable dimensions when a modification
  is requested; the wall to be modified not yet at the limit of ACC; modifications done unsatisfactorily.
- **-1 each**: any denied modification request; unprofessional appearance; universal-precautions violation;
  inadequate isolation; poor operator or patient position.
- Continuing to use a dropped/contaminated instrument without telling the Chief Examiner = 100-point penalty. [CM p.12]

Restorative-specific rules [CM p.22-25]:

- **Lesion diagnosis first**: each procedure starts with a radiograph of the carious lesion; the candidate enters a
  diagnosis and treatment plan. First misdiagnosis -15 points and a second try; second misdiagnosis -100 (that
  procedure fails).
- **Rubber dam ("isolation dam") required** for every restorative procedure: placed before the prep starts, kept
  until the restoration is done, at least one tooth on each side included, intact, with a clear view of the whole
  prep; it must be on whenever the typodont goes to the evaluation area; may come off only to check occlusion and
  must be off for grading of the finished restoration.
- Wedges, bite blocks and sectional matrices are allowed but must be removed before the typodont goes for grading.
- **Simulated caries** = soft enough that a sharp explorer with light pressure sticks and shows "tug-back".
- **Modification requests**: to extend any wall beyond the ACC range (to chase caries), first bring the whole prep
  within ACC and the wall in question to the maximum ACC limit, then state What / Where / Why / How much (e.g.
  extend the gingival wall, caries, 1.0 mm). Denials are not explained.
- **Unsupported enamel**: usually candidate error. If removing it keeps the prep within limits, just remove it (no
  request); if the wall is already over-extended, a request to remove it is denied.
- **Pulp exposure**: if you find one, report it (location and size) before grading. Reporting one that is not there
  = -100; an exposure examiners find that you did not report = -100. Pulp caps are not placed for this exam.
- Composite must be presented without surface glaze or sealer. [CM p.27]

### 4.5 Endodontic exam: anterior procedure, tooth #8 [EA; CM p.13-15]

Procedure: lingual access, canal shaping and obturation on a plastic #8 built with the pulp anatomy of a
21-year-old. Rules: a radiograph gives the tooth length (no measuring); separate rubber dam for each procedure;
clamp only adjacent teeth (or ligate), never the treated tooth; **irrigate the anterior canal with water only**
(chemical irrigants can make the work ungradable and force a retake); obturate with **pink or colored
gutta-percha** (white is hard to tell from sealer); warm or carrier-based thermoplastic gutta-percha is allowed but
discouraged because it can damage the plastic tooth. [CM p.15]

Critical errors (automatic fail): procedure not attempted; wrong tooth; **crown reduced**; any part of the tooth
fractured during instrumentation or obturation; **any perforation**.

| Criterion | ACC | DEF (critical) |
|---|---|---|
| Access placement | On the lingual surface right over the chamber; pulp horns fully removed; straight-line access to the canal | Not over the chamber, or chamber not fully debrided / canal not reachable |
| Access size | Incisal edge of the opening at least 2.0 mm from the incisal edge; cervical edge at least 3.0 mm from the lingual CEJ (cingulum supported); mesiodistal width no more than half the lingual surface (marginal ridges about 1 mm, supported); pulp horns removable | Less than 2.0 mm from the incisal edge; less than 3.0 mm from the lingual CEJ; marginal ridge(s) 1 mm or less; opening too small to remove pulp horns |
| Internal form | Tapers from the lingual surface to the canal opening; slight ledges acceptable | Excessive gouges weakening the tooth |
| Canal, cervical third | Continuous taper; right location and size for apical access | Grossly over-prepared |
| Canal, mid-root | Blends smoothly with the cervical third, no ledges or shoulders | Significant irregularities that will spoil obturation |
| Canal, apical third | Prepared to the anatomical apex or up to 2.0 mm short | Prepared past the apex; transported so the apical canal is not instrumented; more than 2.0 mm short |
| Obturation length | Gutta-percha at the anatomical apex or up to 2.0 mm short | **More than 0.5 mm past the apex**; more than 2.0 mm short |
| Obturation density | Apical third dense, no voids | Significant voids throughout; no gutta-percha; a material other than gutta-percha |
| Coronal level of fill | Gutta-percha in the canal no more than 3.0 mm apical to the CEJ (measured facially); gutta-percha/sealer in the chamber no more than 2.0 mm coronal to the CEJ | Gutta-percha more than 3.0 mm below the CEJ; gutta-percha/sealer more than 2.0 mm above the CEJ; **restorative material in the chamber** |
| Separated file | None, or one that does not affect obturation | A separated file that prevents obturation or leaves it critically deficient |

Note: the endo sheets have only ACC and DEF rows (no SUB), so every miss here is critical. [EA; EP]

### 4.6 Endodontic exam: posterior procedure, tooth #14 (access and canal identification only) [EP]

Same critical errors as 4.5 (not attempted, wrong tooth, crown reduced, fracture, perforation).

| Criterion | ACC | DEF (critical) |
|---|---|---|
| Access placement | Over the chamber; chamber debrided; straight-line access to **all three canals** | Not over the chamber, or chamber not debrided / not all 3 canals reachable |
| Access size and position | In the mesial triangular pit and central fossa; at least 2.0 mm from the mesial marginal ridge; at least 1.0 mm from the line joining the MB and DB cusp tips; at least 1.0 mm mesial to the distal oblique groove; at least 1.0 mm from the mesiolingual (mesiopalatal) cusp tip | Any of those distances short; **or too small: under 2.5 mm at its widest mesiodistally and/or buccolingually** |
| Depth / roof removal | Entire chamber roof removed | Floor at its center more than 10.0 mm deep from the buccal cavosurface margin; or roof not removed enough to clear all pulp tissue |
| Internal form | At least 1.0 mm of supported lateral tooth structure everywhere; tapers to the orifices, no or slight gouges | Under 1.0 mm of lateral support anywhere; or gross ledges blocking the orifices |
| Adjacent teeth | Damage polishes out | Gross damage needing a restoration |
| Surrounding tissue | Slight damage consistent with the procedure | Gross iatrogenic damage to simulated gingiva/typodont |

Self-check for endo: Is the access on the right tooth, centered over the chamber, and big enough to remove the
pulp horns (anterior) or reach MB, DB and P (posterior)? Did I keep the incisal edge, cingulum and marginal ridges
supported? Is the fill within 2 mm short and no more than 0.5 mm long? Dense apical third? Fill level within the
CEJ window? Any perforation or fracture?

### 4.7 Fixed prosthodontic exam: three crown preparations [PC; PZP; PZM; CM p.13, 16]

Procedures (up to 4 h, any order): **all-ceramic crown prep on #9**; **zirconia crown preps on #5 and #3 as the
anterior and posterior abutments of one 3-unit bridge** (so #4 is the pontic site). [CM p.4, 13]
Rules: putty matrices / reduction guides from the provided matrix former may be made during setup without gloves
(one for the ceramic prep, sectioned faciolingually; two for the two zirconia preps, one sectioned mesiodistally and
one faciolingually); all matrices, impressions and models are handed in; analog impressions and poured models are
allowed if the site permits (CDC infection control applies); **digital scanning is prohibited**; **no occlusal
equilibration** of typodont teeth; rubber dam not required. Taper over 16 degrees is critically deficient; the two
bridge abutments must share a common line of draw. [CM p.16]

Critical errors (all three): wrong tooth or surface; procedure not attempted.

| Criterion | ACC | SUB | DEF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Margin extension **[3-SUB]** | From 0.5 mm below to 1.5 mm above the simulated free gingival margin | More than 0.5 mm subgingival | More than 0.5 mm subgingival **and visibly damaging the typodont gingiva**; or more than 1.5 mm supragingival (under-extended) |
| Margin definition | Continuous; may be slightly rough or ill-defined | | No continuity/definition; **beveled margin**; **cupped or J-shaped margin** (lip of unsupported structure) |
| Margin (cervical) width **[3-SUB]** | 0.5-1.5 mm | Over 1.5 up to 2.0 mm | Over 2.0 mm or under 0.5 mm |
| Line of draw **[3-SUB]** | Within 20 degrees of the long axis | 20 to under 30 degrees | 30 degrees or more |
| Axial reduction (ceramic: axial/lingual) **[3-SUB]** | 1.0-2.0 mm | Over 2.0 up to 2.5 mm | Over 2.5 mm or under 1.0 mm |
| Axial walls / undercut | May be slightly rough | | An undercut that, once blocked out, would ruin margin width, and/or deeper than 0.5 mm |
| Taper (per wall) **[3-SUB]** | Nearly parallel up to 12 degrees | Over 12 up to 16 degrees | Over 16 degrees (grossly over-reduced) |
| Incisal reduction (#9) **[3-SUB]** | 1.0-3.0 mm | Over 3.0 up to 3.5 mm | Over 3.5 mm or under 1.0 mm |
| Occlusal reduction (#5, #3) **[3-SUB]** | 1.0-2.0 mm | Over 2.0 up to 2.5 mm | Over 2.5 mm or under 1.0 mm |
| Line angles | Ceramic: external/internal angles rounded (may be irregular). Zirconia: internal angles and cusp-tip areas need not be fully rounded | | Excessively sharp, no rounding |
| Lingual wall height (#9 only) | At least 1.0 mm | | Under 1.0 mm |
| **Bridge factor** (#3/#5 only) | A direct path of insertion, or one that only needs a tilt from vertical to seat fully | | No path of insertion in any plane without cutting more tooth in the apical two-thirds of either prep |
| Adjacent / opposing teeth | Damage polishes out | Adjacent damage needing recontouring that changes the contact; minimal damage to opposing teeth | Gross adjacent damage needing a restoration; gross damage to opposing teeth |
| Surrounding tissue | Slight damage consistent with the procedure | Iatrogenic damage not explained by the procedure | Gross iatrogenic damage |

Self-check: margin between 0.5 mm subgingival and 1.5 mm supragingival, continuous, unbeveled, not cupped, 0.5-1.5
mm wide? Axial reduction 1-2 mm, no undercut? Taper no more than 12 degrees per wall (16 is the hard stop)?
Incisal 1-3 mm (#9) or occlusal 1-2 mm (#3, #5)? Rounded internal angles? Lingual wall at least 1 mm on #9? Do
#3 and #5 share a path of insertion? Pontic-side neighbors and the opposing arch untouched?

Note: the 2026-2027 sheets list ACC ranges that apply to these simulated preps; they are the exam's grading
thresholds, not a full statement of material-specific minimum reductions taught in prosthodontics courses.

### 4.8 Periodontal exam [PR; CM p.10-12]

Up to 90 minutes on the typodont (both arches mounted):

| Task | What is scored | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Calculus detection | 4 assigned **maxillary** teeth x 4 surfaces (M, D, F, L) = 16 surfaces; record calculus present Y/N | 16 (1 each) |
| Calculus removal | One assigned **mandibular** quadrant; 12 assigned key surfaces checked | 66 (5.5 each) |
| Complete calculus removal | All other surfaces in that quadrant: calculus left on 1 surface = -3; on 2 or more = -6 | 6 |
| Probing depths | 2 assigned **mandibular** teeth (1 anterior, 1 posterior) x 6 sites (DF, F, MF, DL, L, ML) = 12 readings; **graded within +/-1 mm** | 12 (1 each) |
| Total | | 100; **75 to pass** |

Tissue management (subtracted from the total):

- **Minor soft-tissue damage** (laceration/abrasion 3 mm or less, no suturing/packing/follow-up needed): -1 per
  site, up to 3 sites. **4 or more minor sites, or 1 major site = automatic failure.**
- **Major soft-tissue damage**: laceration/abrasion over 3 mm that would need sutures, packing or follow-up;
  exposed bone; a flap; an amputated papilla; **an unreported broken instrument tip** in the sulcus or tissue.
- **Minor hard-tissue damage**: slight damage not needing definitive treatment: -1 per site, up to 3.
  **4 or more minor sites, or 1 major site (needs e.g. a restoration) = automatic failure.**
- Dropped/contaminated instrument used without telling the Chief Examiner: 100-point penalty. [CM p.12]
- Findings are recorded on the worksheet, then entered on the examiner's tablet; once checked out of perio, no more
  work on the mandibular arch. [CM p.12]

Arithmetic worth knowing for game design: missing 5 of the 12 key surfaces (-27.5) already drops a perfect score to
72.5, a fail, before any detection or probing errors; key-surface removal is two-thirds of the grade. (Derived from
[PR] point values.)

## 5. Overlap with INBDE

INBDE structure for reference [DoD]: three clinical component sections (Diagnosis and Treatment Planning 36.2%,
Oral Health Management 42.0%, Practice and Profession 21.8%) crossed with 10 foundation knowledge (FK) areas
(e.g. FK8 pharmacology 10.6%, FK6 pathology 11.8%, FK9 behavioral sciences/ethics/jurisprudence 10.6%, FK10
research methods/informatics 9.8%), and 56 numbered clinical content (CC) statements.

### Tagging rule

- **BOTH**: clinical diagnosis and treatment planning items (a patient box, radiograph or photo, "most likely
  diagnosis / next step / what complicates treatment"). This is most of the DLOSCE outline.
- **ADEX**: hand-skill grading criteria (section 4 thresholds, 3-SUB rule, penalties, modification requests),
  prep/restoration critique on 3D models ("which describes this preparation"), and prescription-writing tasks
  with strength, quantity, dose and frequency. The INBDE asks pharmacology, but not as a 4-part prescription.
- **INBDE**: foundation science (FK1-FK7, FK10), ethics/jurisprudence/practice management (CC39-CC55), research
  and evidence appraisal, community and public health. None of these appear in the DLOSCE outline [DLM p.4].

### Area map

prescription tasks and (b) "spot the critical error" prep/restoration critique, which is also the natural game
content. Item-format note: INBDE items have one keyed answer [IG], while DLOSCE uses select-one-or-more with a
penalty for any wrong pick [DLM p.7-8], so ADEX-tagged practice should include that format.
