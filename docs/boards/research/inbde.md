## 1. Format, scoring, eligibility and logistics

### 1.1 What the exam is

- A written, computer-based, cognitive licensure exam owned by the Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations
  (JCNDE, an agency of the ADA), run by the ADA Department of Testing Services (DTS) and delivered by Prometric in the
  US and Canada (CG p. 2). It does not test hands-on clinical skill (FS p. 1).
- It replaced NBDE Part I and Part II. First scored administration August 1, 2020; last NBDE Part I July 31, 2020;
  last NBDE Part II in 2022 (HIST timeline; FS p. 1).
- Every US licensing jurisdiction (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, US Virgin Islands) recognizes it (CG p. 2; LAND).
  It is no longer offered in Quebec (LAND).
- Its content is the **Domain of Dentistry**: 56 Clinical Content (CC) areas crossed with 10 Foundation Knowledge (FK)
  areas, so basic, behavioral and clinical science are tested together inside clinical tasks (CG p. 5; DoD p. 1).

### 1.2 Structure and timing

From the administration schedule (CG p. 24):

| | **Total** | | **12 h 30 min** |

- **500 items** in all: 300 standalone (sections 1-3) + 200 case-based (sections 4-6) (sum of the table above, CG
  p. 24); the technical report states the same 60% standalone / 40% case split (TR p. 18). Pace: 105 minutes per 100 standalone items is about 63 seconds per item; case sections give 105 minutes for
  60 or 70 items (90-105 seconds per item) because the patient box and images are read once per set. Item writers are
  told candidates will usually have one minute or less per item, with the first item of a set as the exception (IDG
  p. 20).
- The two days need not be consecutive, but day 2 must fall within 7 days of day 1 and at the same test center;
  otherwise the candidate retests at their own expense (CG p. 24).
- The on-screen timer pauses while an item loads (CG p. 25). Unscheduled breaks are allowed but the clock runs and
  no lockers, food, phone or notes (CG p. 25).
- Items appear one at a time on screen (CG p. 25). Some items are unscored pretest items that look identical to scored
  ones (CG p. 18; RES).
- Test center: two non-graph note boards and two fine-tip markers are supplied, erased at the end of each day; no
  watches, no magnifiers, no jewelry other than wedding/engagement rings (CG p. 36). Identity is captured biometrically
  and sessions are video- and audio-recorded (CG p. 27). Two current IDs are required (one government photo ID with
  signature, one with signature), and the name must match the application exactly (CG p. 27; PREP checklist).
- Candidates cannot zoom images or change their contrast in the test center (IDG p. 26). This matters for how the hub
  shows radiographs: no zoom in exam mode.
- A Prometric "Test Drive" (30 minutes, including a 15-minute generic sample test, not INBDE content) is available for
  a fee (CG p. 17).

### 1.3 Item types and formats

- **All items are multiple choice with one correct or best answer**, and each has **at least 3 and at most 5 options**
  (CG p. 12). The item-writing guide says most items have 4 options; 3 are used when no other plausible options exist;
  5 is the maximum (IDG p. 13). All 39 official sample items have 4 options (A-D) (PQ, counted).
- There are **no multiple-answer (select-all), matching, ordering or free-response items**: the exam "relies
  exclusively on multiple-choice items" (IDG p. 21; CG p. 12). "All of the above" and "none of the above" are never
  used (IDG p. 15). Options may be words, phrases, sentences, numbers or images (IDG p. 13); one official sample item
  has graphs as its options (PQ, sample 10).
- **Stem styles** (CG p. 12-13): sentence completion (the stem is the start of a sentence that each option finishes)
  and direct questions. **Negatively worded** items use capitalized EXCEPT, LEAST or NOT; writers are told to use them
  sparingly (CG p. 13; IDG p. 12).
- **Presentation types** (IDG p. 21): standalone without a patient box; standalone with a patient box and/or image;
  **itemset** = 3 to 6 items sharing a patient box only; **case** = 3 to 6 items sharing a patient box plus an image or
  other stimulus (photograph, radiograph, lab report, chart, drawing, prescription). NBDE testlets had ten or more items;
  INBDE sets are deliberately small. There is no fixed quota of standalone vs. set items in the item-writing rules
  (IDG p. 21), but the operational form fixes 300 standalone and 200 case items (CG p. 24).
- **Images**: only the images needed for the question are shown, not a full case workup (IDG p. 26). Radiographs carry
  right/left markers; teeth are not numbered on photos or radiographs; missing teeth on charts are blanked out rather
  than X-ed (IDG p. 26). Teeth are written "tooth 27", not "#27" (IDG p. 20). Items written to CC7 (interpret
  diagnostic results) should use a real clinical image, not a drawing (IDG p. 20). The candidate guide shows the two
  dental chart styles used: a line-drawing chart with two rows of probing depths (initial and 1 month after scaling and
  root planing) plus furcation and caries symbols, and a photographic-style "Adult Clinical Examination Chart" with a
  legend (fractured, mobility I-III, impaction, planned extraction, caries, furcation class I-III) (CG p. 15).
- Since 2023 the word "film" is not used for radiographs and darkroom/chemical processing is not tested (UPD p. 4).
- An acronyms and abbreviations list is published for the exam (CG p. 16;
  https://jcnde.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/jcnde/files/inbde_acronymsandabbreviations.pdf).
- **Cognitive levels** each item is classified by (IDG p. 28): Level 1 understanding/recall, Level 2 application,
  Level 3 reasoning/analysis. Each item is also tagged with one FK and one CC for the blueprint (it may relate to
  several), the concept tested, and item statistics (IDG p. 27).

### 1.4 Scoring

- Criterion-referenced; the pass mark is set by subject-matter experts through standard setting and reviewed
  periodically (CG p. 18).
- Reported as **pass/fail**. Passing candidates see only "pass" (CG p. 18).
- Underneath is a **scale score from 49 to 99; 75 is the minimum pass**. A scale score is not a raw count of correct
  answers (CG p. 18). When the standard changes, the scale is adjusted so that 75 still means minimum pass (CG p. 18).
- Pass/fail rests only on the overall scale score. Failing candidates get their overall scale score plus performance
  information for the 3 clinical component sections and the 10 FK areas (CG p. 18).
- Scoring uses item difficulty, item quality and susceptibility to guessing (that is, an item response model); there is
  **no penalty for guessing** (CG p. 18; RES). Forms are equated to a common scale (CG p. 18).
- Results usually arrive within three weeks, with a four-week target (CG p. 19); the results page says about three to
  four weeks (RES). Results of candidates from accredited programs are shared with the dean (CG p. 19). A score cannot
  be voided by the candidate once any part is taken (CG p. 19).
- Results audit: must be requested within 30 days of the report date and takes 4-6 weeks (CG p. 19). The fee is **$105**
  in the guide (CG p. 22) but **$65** on the home page (LAND); treat the guide as current and the page as stale.
- The ADA does not publish a recommended raw percentage to aim for. Any "% correct needed to pass" figure is
  **UNVERIFIED** and must not appear in the hub.
- **New, stricter standard since June 1, 2024.** A Bookmark standard-setting panel met in January 2024; JCNDE approved
  the new standard in May 2024 and applied it from June 1, 2024 (TR p. 31-32).
- **Failure rates** (TR p. 43, Table 16.1), CODA-accredited first-time takers: 1.0% (2020), 1.3% (2021), 0.8% (2022),
  0.4% (2023), then **4.8% (2024)** and **7.2% (2025)** after the new standard (N = 6,816 in 2025). CODA retakers
  failed 27.0% in 2025; all candidates together 20.8% in 2025. The exam is no longer a formality for US students,
  which is a fair, sourced point for the hub to make.
- Reliability for 2025 (6,757 candidates): classification accuracy .96-.97, classification consistency .95-.96
  (TR p. 33); KR-20 .88-.91 (TR p. 35).
- How many of the 500 items are scored vs. unscored pretest items is not published: **UNVERIFIED**. (Field-test
  projections assumed a form with 400 scored items would be highly reliable, TR p. 9; that is not a statement of the
  live count.)

### 1.5 Eligibility and timing in dental school

- A current student at a CODA-accredited program is eligible when the dean (or designee) confirms the student is
  prepared in all applicable disciplines (CG p. 20). Dental school deans decide eligibility for their students (CG
  p. 21). Each school decides when its students are ready (FS p. 2).
- Steps: get a DENTPIN, apply online, receive an eligibility email from DTS, then schedule with Prometric (CG p. 26;
  LAND). Once the application is processed, the candidate has a **six-month eligibility window** (CG p. 26); one
  45-day extension per application costs $150 (CG p. 22-23). The home page advises scheduling 60 to 90 days before the
  desired date (LAND).
- Field tests were given to third- and fourth-year students (HIST timeline 2016) and to the dental class of 2018
  (TR p. 9), which matches the exam's design as an end-of-program test.
- Typical timing for Sam's class: the decisions file records "roughly August 2028" (Sam, not certain;
  `docs/boards/DECISIONS.md`). Nationally, the standard timing (late D3 or D4 year) is set by each school and is not
  stated in the JCNDE documents: **UNVERIFIED**; UAB's own policy should be confirmed with the school.

### 1.6 Fees (CG p. 22-23)

- Examination fee **$890** (includes reports to the candidate, up to three licensing jurisdictions named at
  application, and the dental program).
- Optional INBDE + DLOSCE bundle **$1,080** ("save $470"). Note: the DLOSCE is now licensed to ADEX and all future
  DLOSCE administrations run through ADEX (UPD p. 3), so this bundle may not survive; check before quoting it.
- Extra results report $55 each; results audit $105 (see 1.4); eligibility extension $150; international processing
  fee $435.
- Rescheduling (through Prometric): $50 at 30+ business days, $70 at 5-29, $150 at 1-4 business days (CG p. 23; LAND).
  No-show, or more than 30 minutes late and refused, forfeits the whole fee (CG p. 23). Requests must be made at least
  24 hours ahead (LAND).

### 1.7 Retake rules (CG p. 21)

- Someone who passed may not retake unless a board or regulator requires it.
- **5 years / 5 attempts rule**: pass within five years of the first attempt or within five attempts, whichever comes
  first; after that, one attempt every six months. Not appealable.
- **Wait at least 60 days** between unsuccessful attempts, and **no more than 4 attempts in any 12 months**; after 4 in
  12 months, the next waits until 12 months after the first of them. Not appealable.
- An attempt counts once the candidate has accepted the confidentiality statement at the test computer.
- All past NBDE Part I, Part II and INBDE attempts appear on score reports.
- History: the 2019 fact sheets said 90 days between attempts and a one-year wait after a third failure (FS p. 3);
  that policy is **superseded** by the 2026 guide.

Other points to watch:

- The UPD list held no "forthcoming" (not yet implemented) INBDE change as of 9/23/2026 (UPD p. 4-5). Re-read it
  before each release of the hub; JCNDE tells candidates to check it often (LAND).
- Items are reviewed every year by subject-matter experts to keep up with current research and guidelines (CG p. 16).
  Exam keys therefore follow current guidelines, sometimes with a lag; the hub should cite the guideline version the
  UPD list names.
- The pass standard was raised on **June 1, 2024** (TR p. 32) and first-time CODA failure rates rose from 0.4% (2023)
  to 7.2% (2025) (TR p. 43). Another standard review before 2028 is possible: JCNDE reviews standards periodically
  (CG p. 18).
- From the 2025 to the 2026 guide: exam fee $880 to $890, audit $65 to $105, extension $145 to $150, extra report
  $50 to $55; after the 5-year/5-attempt limit the wait dropped from 12 months to 6 months; and after 4 attempts in 12
  months the next attempt waits for 12 months from the first of them (CG25 p. 21-22 vs CG p. 21-22). The August 2026
  revision added ProGuardian workstation monitoring with audio recording (CG p. 27).
- The blueprint (Domain of Dentistry and test specifications) dates from **July 2018** and rests on the **2016
  practice analysis** (TR p. 15-17). The TR says NBE content specifications rest on practice analyses run roughly
  every five years, with minor changes by test constructors in between (TR p. 2, 17). A new analysis is therefore due
  and could change weights before 2028. The 2026 guide repeats the 2018 lists with tiny wording edits (CC14 says "sex" where
  2018 said "gender"; added serial commas) and rounds the weights to whole percents (CG p. 5-11). No new practice
  analysis or specification change had been announced as of 2026-10-04: **watch item**.
- The DLOSCE moved to ADEX in 2026 (UPD p. 3). This changes the clinical-exam side (see the ADEX research file), not
  the INBDE.
- The 2026 guide's fee and retest tables differ from older fact sheets (FS 2019) and from the home page's audit fee
  (LAND). Always quote the current guide.

## 2. The Domain of Dentistry

### 2.1 Foundation Knowledge areas (FK1-FK10)

Titles exactly as in CG p. 10 (the 2018 TS p. 2 wording differs only in punctuation: FK5 ends "mechanisms"; FK7 has
no leading "The"). Descriptions are my paraphrase of FKD, which gives each area a statement, example disciplines,
example clinical sciences and numbered topics (FKD pages as cited).

**FK1. Molecular, biochemical, cellular, and systems-level development, structure, and function** (FKD p. 2-5).
Normal structure and function from molecules to organ systems, applied to oral care. Covers cell and tissue types;
gross, head-and-neck and dental anatomy; oral histology and embryology (teeth, periodontium, mucosa, salivary glands,
TMJ, muscles of mastication and facial expression); collagen and mineralized-tissue biochemistry and
calcium/phosphate metabolism; membranes and neurotransmission; cell signaling; energy metabolism and macromolecule
synthesis; cell cycle and cell death; organ-system physiology and homeostatic feedback (FKD topics 1.1-1.8).
Disciplines named: anatomy, dental anatomy, occlusion/TMJ, histology, embryology, physiology, cell and molecular
biology, biochemistry, genetics, neuroscience, nutrition, oral biology (FKD p. 2).

**FK2. Physics and chemistry to explain normal biology and pathobiology** (FKD p. 6-8). Physical and chemical
reasoning applied to the body: gas exchange and hemoglobin/oxygen/iron; effects of atmospheric pressure changes;
enamel and dentin dissolution and stability (pH, erosion, abrasion, attrition); physical forces causing hard- and
soft-tissue trauma and tissue responses; ergonomics and work-related musculoskeletal injury (FKD topics 2.1-2.5).

**FK3. Physics and chemistry to explain the characteristics and use of technologies and materials** (FKD p. 9-10).
Radiation physics and radiobiology and the use of radiation in diagnosis and treatment; dental material properties,
biocompatibility and clinical performance; lasers and their tissue interactions (FKD topics 3.1-3.3). Clinical
settings named include prosthodontics, restorative, radiology, implants, endodontics and esthetics (FKD p. 9).

**FK4. Principles of genetic, congenital, and developmental diseases and conditions and their clinical features to
understand patient risk** (FKD p. 11). Inherited disease transmission and its oral/craniofacial features; congenital
(non-inherited) and developmental conditions and how they change care (FKD topics 4.1-4.2). Clinical settings named
include oral pathology and medicine, orthodontics, pediatric dentistry, oral surgery, periodontics, radiology and
cariology (FKD p. 11).

**FK5. Cellular and molecular bases of immune and non-immune host defense mechanisms** (FKD p. 12-13). The immune
system in health and disease: self vs non-self, tolerance, hypersensitivity and autoimmunity; hematopoiesis and the
immune cell lineages; defense against intracellular and extracellular microbes and the basis of vaccines and
immunotherapy (FKD topics 5.1-5.3).

**FK6. General and disease-specific pathology to assess patient risk** (FKD p. 14-15). Cell injury and the natural
history of disease; inflammation and repair; hemostasis and thrombosis (platelets, endothelium, coagulation); how
systemic conditions change dental treatment; mechanisms and dental implications of common metabolic and systemic
diseases (FKD topics 6.1-6.5). This is the "medically complex patient" engine of the exam.

**FK7. The biology of microorganisms in physiology and pathology** (FKD p. 16-17). Host-pathogen interactions,
pathogen structure, transmission, natural history and pathogenesis (and therefore infection control); epidemiology
for individual and community oral health; symbiosis and the oral microbiome in health and disease (FKD topics
7.1-7.3). Disciplines named include microbiology, virology, mycology, parasitology, oral epidemiology and public
health (FKD p. 16).

**FK8. Pharmacology** (FKD p. 18). Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of the major drug classes and OTC products
for safe prescribing; choosing optimal drug therapy for oral conditions from research, the dental literature and
regulation (FKD topics 8.1-8.2). Clinical settings named include cariology, periodontics, endodontics, oral surgery,
pediatric and preventive dentistry, community dentistry (FKD p. 18).

**FK9. Behavioral sciences, ethics, and jurisprudence** (FKD p. 19-20). Sociology, psychology and ethics applied to:
culturally diverse patients; communication with children, adults, older adults and patients with special needs;
fear, anxiety and acute/chronic pain; changing health behavior in individuals and communities; and practice
management decisions (delivery of care, instruments, materials) (FKD topics 9.1-9.5). Named areas include cultural
competence, ergonomics, nutrition counseling, tobacco cessation, access to care and patient compliance (FKD p. 19).

**FK10. Research methodology and analysis, and informatics tools** (FKD p. 21-22). Basic quantitative tools (graphs,
measurement, scales); epidemiology and statistics for disease risk, cause and prognosis; information systems and
literature retrieval; health informatics and data quality; the scientific method, study design, and association vs
causation for evidence-based decisions (FKD topics 10.1-10.5).

## 3. Cases, patient boxes and item style

### 3.1 The patient box

The patient box is a fixed-format panel of patient information shown **beside** the question (box in a left column,
stem and options on the right in the official samples and model items: PQ; IDGA PDF p. 34-90; HIST mentions the
"side-by-side presentation"). It holds what the dentist would know at that visit. **A blank area means unknown or not
available; for example, no listed allergies means no known allergies** (CG p. 14). Candidates are told to always use
it (CG p. 14; IDG p. 24).

It has exactly four headed areas, always in this order (CG p. 14; IDG p. 22-23, Figures 6-7):

| Area (heading as shown) | Required? | What goes in it (IDG p. 23; CG p. 14) | Writing conventions (IDG p. 24-25) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Patient** | Required | Sex and age ("Female, 28 years old" style); ethnicity only if relevant | Must agree with any photo (e.g., a bearded patient is presented as male) |
| **Chief Complaint** | Required | The main symptom or reason for the visit in the patient's (or guardian's) own words, with its duration; never the diagnosis | First person, in quotation marks; if a parent speaks, it is attributed to them; patients use brand names, so brand names may appear here |
| **Background and/or Patient History** | May be blank | In this order: medical conditions; current medications; other treatments; history of dental diagnoses and treatment; allergies; social history (tobacco, occupation, living situation of an older patient) | One condition or medication per line; drugs as generic name first, then brand in parentheses with the (R) mark; dose only when it matters (aspirin is the stated example); diabetes always typed (type 1 or type 2); a source other than the dentist is named |
| **Current Findings** | May be blank | What the dental team found at this visit: height and weight (optional), vital signs (e.g., blood pressure, glucose), diagnostic test results, and an assessment such as swelling, bleeding sites or maximum opening | Vitals and tests may be summarized as "stable" or "within normal limits"; height/weight written like 6' 1", 230 lbs; BP and Temp abbreviated |

For the hub: the official box has **no separate fields** for medications, allergies, social history, vitals or dental
history; they are lines inside "Background and/or Patient History" and "Current Findings". The hub's case renderer
should use the same four headings in the same order so students get used to the real layout.

Rules JCNDE gives its writers that the hub should copy (IDG p. 20, 24-25):

- Put the information in the box, not repeated in the stem or options. Do not write "this patient" in the stem.
- Include some realistic but irrelevant data: a box that shows blood pressure only when blood pressure matters gives
  the answer away. Students must learn to find the signal in the noise.
- Every fact in the box must agree with the stem, images, options and key (an allergy may change the right drug; a
  distractor about quitting smoking needs a smoker in the box).
- In a set, the box is shared by all items, so it holds what every item needs; extra facts for one item go in that
  item's stem (IDG p. 25). In the model cases, a later item moves the story on (the patient returns days or weeks
  later with a new sign) (IDGA PDF p. 62-90).
- Tooth numbers are Universal and written "tooth 27"; photos and radiographs are not labeled with tooth numbers;
  radiographs carry R/L markers (IDG p. 20, 26).

How often each part is used: 25 of the 39 official sample items carry a patient box; by a keyword count, about 13 of
those list medications, about 11 mention past dental treatment, 6 have vital signs, about 6 have a parent or guardian
speaking, 4 give lab values and 3 list allergies (counted from PQ; approximate).

### 3.2 Cases and item sets

- **Share of the exam**: 200 of 500 items (40%) are case items, in section 4 (day 1) and sections 5-6 (day 2) (CG
  p. 24; TR p. 18).
- **Size**: the item-writing guide defines an *itemset* as 3-6 items sharing a patient box only, and a *case* as 3-6
  items sharing a patient box plus an image or other stimulus (photograph, radiograph, lab report, chart, drawing,
  prescription) (IDG p. 21). The technical report describes a case as **2 to 5 items** sharing a stimulus, of two kinds:
  single-stimulus cases (one image or a patient box) and comprehensive multiple-image cases (patient box, dental
  charting, radiographs, photographs) (TR p. 18). Both are official documents; plan for **2-6 items per case, typically
  3-5**. NBDE testlets often ran ten or more items; INBDE sets are deliberately short (IDG p. 21).
- **Stimulus**: only the images needed for the items are shown, not a full records set (IDG p. 26). Charts follow the
  two styles shown in CG p. 15 (see 1.3).
- **What case items ask**: the TR lists the tasks: interpret the findings, identify problems and diagnose, select
  materials, technique and armamentarium, apply treatment, evaluate progress and complications, and set up
  prevention and maintenance (TR p. 18-19). Items in one case can come from any CC, Practice and Profession included,
  and any FK (TR p. 18). The 12 case items in the published model set (4 cases of 2-5 items each, IDGA PDF p. 62-90)
  mix this way within one patient: identifying a structure on a radiograph, the likely cause of an oral or throat
  finding, the mechanism behind a soft-tissue condition, a periodontal relationship, an adverse reaction after a
  physician's prescription, the drug for an opportunistic infection, how to answer a patient's question about
  replacing teeth, and EXCEPT-style treatment-plan and patient-management items (described, not copied).
- **Timing**: case sections allow about 90-105 seconds per item versus about 63 seconds for standalones (computed from
  CG p. 24); the first item of a set takes longest because the box and images are read then (IDG p. 20).
- Standalone items may also carry a patient box and/or an image (IDG p. 21); most of the sample standalones do (PQ).

### 3.3 Style of the official sample and model items (described, not copied)

Sources: the 39 sample questions (PQ, 2025), the format examples in CG p. 12-13, and the 2019 model items in IDGA
Appendix D (29 "model items" plus 12 case items and 17 standalone items).

- **Options**: one best answer; 3-5 options allowed, 4 in most items (CG p. 12; IDG p. 13). All 39 PQ items have 4
  (A-D); the model items are mostly 4, a few 5 (IDGA). Options are short (often one to five words: a drug, a diagnosis,
  a structure, a tooth number), grammatically parallel and about equal in length, so the key does not stand out as
  the longest or most specific (IDG p. 13-14, 17). Default order is alphabetical or chronological, otherwise logical
  (IDG p. 20). "All of the above" and "none of the above" are never used (IDG p. 15). Options can be images or graphs
  (IDG p. 13; PQ sample 10).
- **Stems**: short. Across the PQ items, a whole item (patient box + stem + options) has a median of about 66 words
  (range about 8 to 112) (counted from PQ). The stem itself is usually one sentence; the clinical detail sits in the
  box. 36 of 39 PQ stems are direct questions, the rest sentence completions (counted from PQ; both forms are
  official, CG p. 12). Each item tests one concept: diagnosis or treatment, drug or dose, not both (IDG p. 20).
- **Lead-ins**: most likely diagnosis or cause; best or most appropriate treatment, management or next step; the
  mechanism of a drug or disease; drug of choice; what a finding or image shows; the most appropriate response to the
  patient (PQ; IDGA). "Best response" items are favored for higher-level thinking (IDG p. 13).
- **Negatively worded items**: EXCEPT, LEAST or NOT, always in capitals; the EXCEPT form reads "Each of the following
  ... EXCEPT one. Which is the EXCEPTION?" (CG p. 13). Writers use them sparingly (IDG p. 12): 7 of 39 PQ items
  (about 18%) are negative, 6 EXCEPT and 1 LEAST (counted from PQ).
- **Images**: 6 of 39 PQ items carry an image (radiographs including a panoramic, clinical photographs, graphs)
  (counted from PQ). On the real exam most images sit in case sections (IDG p. 21; TR p. 18). Candidates cannot zoom
  or adjust images (IDG p. 26).
- **Integration**: each PQ item is tagged with 1-3 FKs and 1-5 CCs (see 2.3): a biomedical fact is asked inside a
  clinical decision (for example, a drug mechanism asked through a medicated patient's complaint).
- **Cognitive level**: items are classed as recall, application or reasoning/analysis (IDG p. 28) and aimed at the
  "just qualified candidate", so that minimally competent candidates get them right and those just below do not
  (IDG p. 20-21).
- **Language**: plain, no regionalisms or slang; behavioral-science jargon is replaced by a description (IDG p. 20);
  fair to all groups (IDG p. 18; IDGA Appendix C fairness checklist). Drugs: generic first, brand in parentheses;
  current drugs only (IDG p. 24). Abbreviations come from the official list (CG p. 16).
- JCNDE says practice questions only show formats, may be outdated and are not a content guide (CG p. 16-17). They are
  copyrighted and may not be reproduced (CG p. 17; PREP): **the hub must never copy them**.

### 4.3 Bank-level rules (cross-cutting integration)

- **Tag every item** with: unit, one primary CC, one primary FK (secondary CCs/FKs allowed, as JCNDE does, IDG p. 27),
  cognitive level 1-3 (IDG p. 28), standalone or case, image yes/no, and the outline version
  ("INBDE DoD 2018 / CG 2026-08").
- **Hold the bank to the matrix**: by primary tags, each component x FK cell of the bank should stay within about
  1 percentage point of TS p. 3 (for example, OHM x FK1 6.8%, P&P x FK9 4.8%). The hub's admin/review page can show
  the live matrix next to the target.
- **40% in case sets** to match the exam (TR p. 18): about 480 items in roughly 100-120 cases of 3-5 items. Write
  cases as whole patients that cross units (for example, a medicated patient with periodontitis whose items cover a
  drug mechanism (FK8), a periodontal diagnosis (CC22), an infection-control step (CC51) and how to discuss prognosis
  (CC13)), and tag each item to its own unit.
- **Integration inside clinical items**: put the basic-science step inside a clinical decision rather than asking it
  bare: the mechanism of an anticoagulant behind gingival bleeding (FK8 inside CC8), the embryology behind a
  developmental anomaly on a radiograph (FK4 inside CC7), the microbiology behind a mucosal lesion's treatment (FK7
  inside CC23), the chemistry behind an erosion pattern (FK2 inside CC21), or the statistics behind a caries-risk
  test (FK10 inside CC10). This mirrors how the official samples carry 1-3 FKs and several CCs per item (PQ).
- **Item style** follows section 3: 4 options (3-5 allowed), one best answer, no "all/none of the above", negatives
  in capitals and kept to about 15-20% of items, short stems with the detail in a four-part patient box, irrelevant
  but realistic box data, R/L markers and no tooth labels on images, generic drug names with brand in parentheses.
- **Guideline versions**: write keys to the versions JCNDE says it uses (UPD p. 4-5): 2017 AAP/EFP perio
  classification, 2017 AHA blood pressure, 2021 AHA antibiotic prophylaxis, 2020 ASA physical status, 2020 IADT trauma,
  cigarettes per day, and no film-processing items. Re-check the UPD list before each release.
- **Staging**: units can open in the order the class takes the matching courses (DECISIONS.md), as long as the
  bank-level FK and component quotas are met by the full release.
