# Facts of record: medically complex patients, pharmacology, emergencies

Compiled 2026-10-04 for the boards hub (INBDE + ADEX). Every question and note that touches these topics must match
the statements below. Each fact is paraphrased (no copied text) and cites the source opened, with page or section.
Anything marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from an opened source and must not be used as a keyed answer
until someone verifies it.

How to use: cite the fact id (e.g. `IE-4`) in a question's source note. When a guideline is revised, update the fact
here first, then grep the bank for the id.

## 1. Infective endocarditis prophylaxis (AHA 2021) and prosthetic joints (ADA)

**Sources.** Wilson WR et al. *Prevention of Viridans Group Streptococcal Infective Endocarditis: A Scientific
Statement From the American Heart Association.* Circulation 2021;143:e963-e978, doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000000969
(PubMed 33853363; not in PMC; PDF opened from a public mirror of the published article). ADA Oral Health Topics,
"Antibiotic Prophylaxis Prior to Dental Procedures", https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/antibiotic-prophylaxis
(last updated July 15, 2026).

- **IE-1** The 2021 statement made **no changes** to the 2007 AHA recommendations; prophylaxis is still limited to the
  4 categories at highest risk of a bad outcome from viridans streptococcal IE (Abstract; Table 3, p. e971).
- **IE-2** Conditions for which prophylaxis is suggested (Table 3, p. e971):
  (a) prosthetic cardiac valve or prosthetic material: a prosthetic valve, transcatheter-implanted prosthetic valves,
  valve repair with devices (annuloplasty rings, clips), and LVADs or implantable hearts;
  (b) previous, relapsed or recurrent IE;
  (c) congenital heart disease: unrepaired cyanotic CHD (including palliative shunts and conduits); a CHD defect
  completely repaired with prosthetic material or a device (surgical or transcatheter) **during the first 6 months**
  after the procedure; repaired CHD with residual defects at or next to a prosthetic patch or device; surgical or
  transcatheter pulmonary artery valve or conduit (e.g., Melody valve, Contegra conduit);
  (d) cardiac transplant recipients who develop valvulopathy.
- **IE-3** Prophylaxis is **not** suggested for (Table 3, p. e971): pacemakers and similar implantable electronic
  devices, septal closure devices once closure is complete, peripheral vascular grafts and patches (including
  hemodialysis grafts), coronary or other vascular stents, CNS ventriculoatrial shunts, vena cava filters, pledgets.
  Rheumatic heart disease, bicuspid aortic valve, mitral valve prolapse and other native valve disease were reviewed
  and **not** added (p. e971, "Feasibility of including additional high-risk groups").
- **IE-4** Procedures (Table 4, p. e972): for the high-risk patients, prophylaxis is suggested for **all dental
  procedures that manipulate gingival tissue or the periapical region of teeth, or perforate the oral mucosa**. Not
  suggested: anesthetic injections through noninfected tissue, radiographs, placing removable prosthodontic or
  orthodontic appliances, adjusting orthodontic appliances, placing orthodontic brackets, shedding of primary teeth,
  bleeding from trauma to the lips or mucosa.
- **IE-5** Regimen = a **single dose 30 to 60 minutes before** the procedure (Table 5, p. e973):

  | Situation | Agent | Adult | Child |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Oral | Amoxicillin | 2 g | 50 mg/kg |
  | Cannot take oral | Ampicillin | 2 g IM or IV | 50 mg/kg IM or IV |
  | Cannot take oral | Cefazolin or ceftriaxone | 1 g IM or IV | 50 mg/kg IM or IV |
  | Penicillin/ampicillin allergy, oral | Cephalexin (or another 1st/2nd-generation oral cephalosporin in equivalent dose) | 2 g | 50 mg/kg |
  | Penicillin/ampicillin allergy, oral | Azithromycin or clarithromycin | 500 mg | 15 mg/kg |
  | Penicillin/ampicillin allergy, oral | Doxycycline | 100 mg | under 45 kg: 2.2 mg/kg; over 45 kg: 100 mg |
  | Allergy and cannot take oral | Cefazolin or ceftriaxone | 1 g IM or IV | 50 mg/kg IM or IV |

- **IE-6** **Clindamycin is no longer recommended** (oral or parenteral) for dental prophylaxis: it causes more frequent
  and severe reactions, including fatal *C. difficile* infection after a single dose; up to 15% of community-acquired
  *C. difficile* may trace to antibiotics prescribed for dental procedures (p. e972; Table 5 footnote, p. e973).
- **IE-7** Do **not** use a cephalosporin in someone whose penicillin/ampicillin reaction was anaphylaxis, angioedema
  or urticaria (Table 5 footnote, p. e973).
- **IE-8** Azithromycin/clarithromycin: caution with a known prolonged QTc (over 450 ms on ECG), torsades risk;
  doxycycline is the alternative when a penicillin, cephalosporin and macrolide can't be used (p. e972). About 90% of
  people labeled penicillin-allergic have negative skin tests; skin testing is suggested if feasible (p. e972).
- **IE-9** Forgot the dose: it **may be given up to 2 hours after** the procedure (p. e973).
- **IE-10** Patient already on a short (7-10 day) antibiotic course: pick an antibiotic from a **different class**;
  if possible, delay elective care until **10 days** after the course ends. Serial appointments: if possible, wait
  10 days after the last dose before the next procedure. Patient on parenteral therapy for IE or another infection:
  the same parenteral antibiotic may cover the procedure (p. e973). ADA example: on amoxicillin, use azithromycin or
  clarithromycin (ADA page, "Additional Considerations").
- **IE-11** Rationale: viridans strep IE is far more likely from daily activities (chewing, toothbrushing, flossing)
  than from dental procedures; prophylaxis may prevent an exceedingly small number of cases. Good oral health and
  regular dental care are emphasized (Abstract, p. e963; ADA page).
- **PJ-1** ADA 2015 clinical practice guideline (Sollecito et al., JADA 2015, from a 2014 systematic review): in
  general, prophylactic antibiotics are **not recommended** before dental procedures to prevent prosthetic joint
  infection; the review found no association between dental procedures and prosthetic joint infection (ADA page,
  "Prevention of Prosthetic Joint Infection").
- **PJ-2** For a patient with a history of complications from the joint replacement surgery who needs procedures with
  gingival manipulation or mucosal incision, prophylaxis is only considered after consulting the patient and the
  orthopedic surgeon; if it is needed, the **orthopedic surgeon should recommend the regimen** and, when reasonable,
  write the prescription (ADA Chairside Guide, cited on the ADA page, Key Points).
- **PJ-3** Newest ADA position (ADA Council on Scientific Affairs, JADA 2026;157(6):667-669): timing of dental care
  and any rare prophylaxis for joint-replacement patients rests on **shared decision making** among dentist,
  orthopedic team and patient, not routine delays or automatic premedication (ADA page, Key Points and ref. 4).
- **PJ-4** Other implants (solid organ transplant, breast implants, penile implants): no general recommendation for
  prophylaxis; only if the treating physician judges the person predisposed to infection, and the physician prescribes
  (ADA page, "Miscellaneous Indications"). Chemotherapy patient with a central venous catheter: consult the oncologist
  about prophylaxis (same section, citing NIDCR).

## 2. Local anesthetics: maximum doses, cartridges, toxicity, methemoglobinemia

**Adult maximums (FDA labels) and mg per cartridge**

| Drug | mg per cartridge | Label maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Lidocaine 2% with epi 1:100,000 or 1:50,000 | 34 mg in 1.7 mL (20 mg/mL) | 7 mg/kg (3.2 mg/lb); keep total below 500 mg in a healthy adult (Xylocaine Dental, Dosage and Administration) |
| Articaine 4% with epi 1:100,000 or 1:200,000 | 68 mg in 1.7 mL (40 mg/mL) | 7 mg/kg articaine (0.175 mL/kg), same for ages 4-16; the label gives **no absolute mg cap** (Septocaine, 2.2) |
| Mepivacaine 3% plain | 51 mg in 1.7 mL | up to 3 mg/lb (= 6.6 mg/kg); never more than 400 mg per sitting in adults (Carbocaine/Polocaine, Dosage and Administration) |
| Mepivacaine 2% with levonordefrin 1:20,000 | 34 mg in 1.7 mL | same: 3 mg/lb, 400 mg per sitting (same sections) |
| Prilocaine 4% plain or with epi 1:200,000 | 72 mg in 1.8 mL (40 mg/mL) | 8 mg/kg (4 mg/lb) under 70 kg (150 lb); at or over 150 lb no more than 600 mg (8 cartridges); limit applies within a 2-hour period (Citanest Plain and Forte, Dosage and Administration) |
| Bupivacaine 0.5% with epi 1:200,000 | 9 mg in 1.8 mL | total for a sitting ordinarily no more than 90 mg in a healthy adult (= ten 1.8 mL cartridges); not recommended under age 12 (Vivacaine, Dosage and Administration; Pediatric Use) |

- **LA-1** The table above is the keyed set. Labels quoted in mg/lb are 3.2 mg/lb (lidocaine), 3 mg/lb (mepivacaine) and
  4 mg/lb (prilocaine). Note US dental lidocaine and mepivacaine cartridges are labeled **1.7 mL**; prilocaine and
  bupivacaine cartridges are **1.8 mL** (labels, How Supplied). Questions that say "1.8 mL cartridge of 2% lidocaine
  = 36 mg" follow the older textbook convention; prefer the label's 34 mg/1.7 mL, or state the volume in the stem.
- **LA-2** Mepivacaine labels: 5.3 cartridges (270 mg of 3% or 180 mg of 2%) is usually enough to anesthetize the
  whole mouth; 1 cartridge usually suffices for an infiltration or block (Carbocaine and Scandonest, Dosage and
  Administration).
- **LA-3** Septocaine epinephrine ceiling: 0.0017 mg/kg epinephrine; 1:100,000 = 0.018 mg/mL and 1:200,000 =
  0.009 mg/mL of epinephrine (Septocaine, 2.2 and 3). Indicated for adults and children **4 years and older**;
  safety below age 4 not established (Septocaine, 1 and 8.4).
- **LA-4** Children under 10: rarely more than half a cartridge per procedure for a single tooth (lidocaine: 0.9-1.0 mL,
  18-20 mg; prilocaine: 40 mg) (Xylocaine Dental and Citanest, Dosage and Administration). Label pediatric maximums use
  a weight formula (Clark's rule); for a 5-year-old weighing 50 lb, the lidocaine label example gives 75-100 mg and
  the prilocaine label 150-200 mg (same sections). Mepivacaine pediatric max = child's weight (lb) / 150 x 400 mg
  (Carbocaine, Dosage and Administration).
- **LA-5** AAPD pediatric maximums (more conservative than labels, adapted from Cote et al.; AAPD Table, p. 408):
  lidocaine **4.4 mg/kg** (2 mg/lb), articaine **7 mg/kg** (3.2 mg/lb), mepivacaine **4.4 mg/kg** (2 mg/lb),
  bupivacaine **1.3 mg/kg** (0.6 mg/lb). The AAPD notes the label (manufacturer) maximum for lidocaine is 7 mg/kg
  (Table footnote C). Reduce the amide maximum by **30% in infants under 6 months** (footnote B; p. 411). Articaine is
  not recommended under age 4; bupivacaine not under age 12 (footnotes D, E). AAPD's table gives no prilocaine row;
  **no AAPD mg/kg value for prilocaine was verified**.
- **LA-6** AAPD vasoconstrictor per 1.7 mL cartridge (Table, p. 408): epi 1:50,000 = 0.034 mg; 1:100,000 = 0.017 mg;
  1:200,000 = 0.0085 mg; levonordefrin 1:20,000 = 0.085 mg. Bupivacaine 0.5% = 8.5 mg per 1.7 mL in the AAPD table
  (the Vivacaine label's 1.8 mL cartridge holds 9 mg).
- **LA-7** Early CNS toxicity signs (labels): restlessness, anxiety, tinnitus, dizziness, blurred vision, tremors,
  depression or drowsiness (Septocaine 5.2; Citanest Plain, Precautions). AAPD (p. 409-410): toxicity is biphasic
  (excitation then depression); early subjective signs are dizziness, anxiety, confusion, then diplopia, tinnitus,
  drowsiness, circumoral numbness/tingling; objective signs are muscle twitching, tremor, talkativeness, slowed
  speech, shivering, then a generalized tonic-clonic seizure (the classic overdose reaction), loss of consciousness and
  respiratory arrest. Cardiovascular: HR and BP may rise at first (epinephrine), then vasodilation, myocardial
  depression, falling BP, bradycardia and arrest. Most reactions come during the injection or within **5-10 minutes**.
- **LA-8** Toxicity management: stop the anesthetic, airway and ventilation first; anticonvulsant if seizures persist
  despite ventilation; CPR if arrest (Septocaine, 10 Overdosage). AAPD: early IV lipid emulsion is a priority in
  potentially serious LAST; activate EMS by severity (p. 410). Prevention: aspirate, inject slowly, calculate the
  maximum by weight (AAPD p. 410). Small dental doses can cause toxic reactions through intra-arterial injection with
  retrograde flow to the brain (Septocaine 5.1; Citanest, Precautions).
- **LA-9** Methemoglobinemia (all LA labels; Septocaine 5.4): any LA can cause it; higher risk with G6PD deficiency,
  congenital or idiopathic methemoglobinemia, cardiac or pulmonary compromise, **infants under 6 months**, and other
  oxidizing drugs (examples in Septocaine Table 5: nitrates/nitrites incl. nitroglycerin and nitrous oxide; dapsone,
  sulfonamides, nitrofurantoin; chloroquine, primaquine; phenytoin, phenobarbital, valproate; acetaminophen,
  metoclopramide). Signs: cyanosis (gray/blue skin, nail beds, lips), abnormally colored blood, headache, tachycardia,
  shortness of breath, fatigue; can appear immediately or hours later. Treat: stop the drug, oxygen, and for severe
  cases **IV methylene blue 1-2 mg/kg over 5 minutes** (Citanest Plain and Forte, Overdosage; Carbocaine, Warnings),
  exchange transfusion or hyperbaric oxygen. Prilocaine's metabolite **o-toluidine** forms methemoglobin (Citanest,
  Clinical Pharmacology); prilocaine is **contraindicated** in congenital or idiopathic methemoglobinemia (Citanest,
  Contraindications). AAPD: benzocaine contraindicated with a history of methemoglobinemia and **under age 2**;
  prilocaine relatively contraindicated in G6PD deficiency, sickle cell anemia, anemia, very young patients or hypoxia
  (pp. 407-408, 411).
- **LA-10** Sulfite: dental LA cartridges with epinephrine contain a bisulfite/metabisulfite antioxidant that can cause
  allergic-type reactions and asthma attacks in sulfite-sensitive people (Xylocaine Dental, Warnings; Septocaine,
  Description). Plain mepivacaine 3% and plain prilocaine 4% contain no vasoconstrictor (labels, Description).
- **LA-11** Paresthesia is reported more often with 4% solutions (articaine, prilocaine) than lower concentrations
  (AAPD p. 410). Phentolamine mesylate (reversal): reduces soft-tissue numbness about 47% (maxilla) and 67% (mandible);
  not recommended under age 3 or under 15 kg (33 lb) (AAPD p. 410).
- **LA-12** Plain prilocaine 4%: about 10 minutes of pulpal anesthesia by maxillary infiltration; by IANB, onset under
  3 minutes and soft-tissue anesthesia about 2.5 hours (Citanest Plain, Clinical Pharmacology).

## 3. Vasoconstrictors in cardiac patients and drug interactions

**Sources.** FDA labels already listed in section 2 (Xylocaine Dental, Precautions/Drug Interactions; Septocaine,
5 Warnings and 7 Drug Interactions; Carbocaine, Precautions). Godzieba A et al. *Clinical assessment of the safe use
local anaesthesia with vasoconstrictor agents in cardiovascular compromised patients: a systematic review.* Med Sci
Monit 2014;20:393-398, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3958566/ . Saraghi M, Golden LR, Hersh EV.
*Anesthetic Considerations for Patients on Antidepressant Therapy, Part I.* Anesth Prog 2017;64(4):253-261,
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5715313/ . Periodontology 2000 review *Illegal drugs and periodontal
conditions* (2022/2023), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828249/ .

- **VC-1** Cardiac dose ceiling: the healthy-adult maximum of epinephrine in dental anesthesia is commonly given as
  **0.2 mg**, lowered to **0.04 mg** for patients with significant cardiovascular disease (ASA III-IV) (Godzieba 2014,
  Background, citing earlier literature). 0.04 mg = about **2 cartridges of 1:100,000** (0.017-0.018 mg each; see LA-3,
  LA-6) or about 4 cartridges of 1:200,000. Keep it as "0.04 mg"; the cartridge count is a derived teaching number.
- **VC-2** The same review concluded that up to **4 cartridges of lidocaine with epinephrine 1:100,000** seemed
  relatively safe in cardiovascular-compromised patients, that most arrhythmias seen were clinically insignificant, and
  that ECG monitoring should be considered at higher doses (Godzieba 2014, Conclusions). AHA/ADA position cited there:
  no contraindication to vasoconstrictor if given carefully with aspiration (Background).
- **VC-3** Label interactions (epinephrine-containing dental LAs): with **MAO inhibitors, nonselective beta-blockers
  or tricyclic antidepressants**, severe prolonged hypertension can occur; **phenothiazines and butyrophenones** may
  reduce or reverse epinephrine's pressor effect; avoid concurrent use, or monitor closely if unavoidable
  (Septocaine 5 and 7). Xylocaine Dental (Precautions): with a beta-blocker (propranolol, timolol), dose-dependent
  **hypertension and bradycardia** with possible heart block. Vasopressors plus **ergot-type oxytocics**: severe
  persistent hypertension or stroke (Xylocaine Dental; Carbocaine, Precautions).
- **VC-4** Mechanism of the nonselective beta-blocker interaction: with beta-2 vasodilation blocked, epinephrine's
  alpha-1 vasoconstriction is unopposed, so BP rises and a reflex bradycardia follows (consistent with the label's
  "hypertension and bradycardia", VC-3). Cardioselective (beta-1) blockers such as metoprolol and atenolol are not
  named in the labels' warning. (Mechanism wording is a teaching paraphrase; only the label effect is sourced.)
- **VC-5** TCAs and SNRIs block norepinephrine reuptake, so injected epinephrine and especially **levonordefrin**
  (mainly alpha effects, so the effect is mostly on BP) can have exaggerated, prolonged effects; minimize the
  epinephrine dose and inject slowly (Saraghi 2017, "Drug interactions" section and Fig. 2). Amphetamines (e.g.,
  Adderall) and atomoxetine share this concern; SSRIs, trazodone, mirtazapine and bupropion do not have significant
  epinephrine interactions (same section).
- **VC-6** MAOIs: pharmacologically no interaction with direct-acting agents like epinephrine is expected, but the
  labels still advise caution (VC-3); the dangerous MAOI interaction is with **indirect-acting** sympathomimetics
  (ephedrine, pseudoephedrine), which can cause hypertensive crisis and are contraindicated (Saraghi 2017, same
  section). Don't stop an MAOI without the psychiatrist.
- **VC-7** Cocaine and other stimulants: they are sympathomimetic, so epinephrine-containing LA, epinephrine
  retraction cord and nitrous oxide may be contraindicated in a patient who has just used cocaine; elective care can
  usually be done **24 hours** after the last stimulant (cocaine, crack, amphetamine) use (Periodontol 2000 review,
  section on dental management and Table 4: "avoid treatment at least 24 h from last drug use", "avoid local
  anesthetic with epinephrine"). Methamphetamine acts for about **8-12 hours** (same review, section 6.3).
- **VC-8** Not every BP/cardiac limit exam stems use is in a current guideline: "0.04 mg" (VC-1) and "defer elective
  care at 180/110" (BP-3) are the keyed numbers for this bank.
