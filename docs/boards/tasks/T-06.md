## Writing rules (from BUILD_SPEC 4.1/4.3 and the benchmark's defects)

1. **Single best answer, 4 options** (3 only when no fourth plausible one exists). No "all/none of the above", no
   "both A and B". 6-12 items (10-20%) negative: EXCEPT / NOT / LEAST in capitals with `fmt` set; the EXCEPT form reads
   "Each of the following ... EXCEPT one. Which is the EXCEPTION?"; never a negative as a case's first item.
2. **Short stem, detail in the box.** Usually one direct question; box + stem + options about 40-110 words. One
   concept per item. Never "this patient". Clinical standalones carry their own `pbox`; a pure recall item may have
   none. Case items never carry a `pbox`.
3. **Box:** exactly Patient / Chief Complaint (the patient's own words in quotes, with a duration, never the
   diagnosis) / Background and/or Patient History (conditions, medications, other treatment, dental history,
   allergies, social, one per line) / Current Findings. **Every number the key needs is in the box or stem**:
   repeated BP readings, INR with its date, platelet and neutrophil counts with units, glucose, weight for any dose,
   the allergy and the reaction it caused ("amoxicillin: hives" vs "amoxicillin: nausea"). Every fact in the box
   agrees with the stem, options and key.
4. **Stay inside the facts.** Read each cited fact's `conditions` before writing: they say what must not be keyed.
   In particular:
   - `stem_names` facts: the stem names the source (`BP-3` ADA; `END-10`/`END-11` Endocrine Society; `CH-2` NIDCR,
     an archived guide, so say so; `CH-5`, `CH-6`, `SCD-6`, `TX-1`, `TX-2`, `TX-4` AAPD; `RES-16` BTS). The lint fails
     the item otherwise.
   - AAPD numbers are pediatric: use them only for a child or teen, never as the adult rule.
   - `AC-2` is partial: key only the 2-3 therapeutic INR range, never when the INR must be checked before surgery.
   - `IE-5` doses rest on the AHA table: an item that keys a dose names the AHA.
   - A hedged fact ("may be considered", "relatively contraindicated", "consult") is never keyed as an absolute.
5. **Options:** same category, grammatically parallel, similar length. The key may be strictly the longest in at most
   35% of items (aim for under 25%), and no authored answer index above 40% (aim for about 15 each of 0-3).
   `fixed: true` only for numeric or logical lists, written in ascending order.
6. **Distractors:** each is a specific error a student would make (an older guideline, a pediatric number for an
   adult, the right drug for the wrong allergy). Absolute words ("always", "never") never only in distractors. A
   distractor that is right under an older guideline (2007 AHA list, JNC 7 BP categories) is fine only when the stem
   names the current one.
7. **Explanations:** `ex` 2-4 sentences, from the box to the key, naming the most tempting wrong answer and why it
   fails. `why[]`: one line per option in `choices` order; the key's line starts "Correct." and gives the deciding
   fact; each distractor's line names the error and the fact that rules it out. `cue` required when `cog` or `diff`
   is 3, and worth adding to most items.
8. **Cases:** each item answerable from the box plus its own stem; later stems may move the story on ("Two weeks
   later, he returns..."). **No leakage:** an earlier item's stem, `ex` or why lines never contain a later item's
   key. When a case uses one concept twice (`c-medcx-0004`, `0005`, `0006`), the two items test different decisions.
   Explanations use only facts in the case.
9. **Calculations** (facts with `data`: `IE-5`, `BLD-1`, `BLD-11`, `LIV-3`, `END-11`, `CH-5`, `CH-6`): add a `calc`
   expression the lint recomputes (see `b-anes-0002`); no distractor may equal the computed value.
10. **Words:** American English; generic drug names in stems and options (brand with ® only in a box, as a patient
    would say it); "tooth 30", never "#30"; no 8+ consecutive words from any fact quote or another item (the lint's
    copy guard); nothing that resembles a prep-company or released exam question.
