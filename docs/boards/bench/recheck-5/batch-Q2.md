# Batch Q2

### R01

**Patient:** Male, 15 years old
**Chief Complaint:** "My back tooth really hurts and my cheek is swollen." (2 days)
**Background and/or Patient History:**
- Acute lymphoblastic leukemia, receiving chemotherapy
- Indwelling central venous catheter
- Ondansetron 4 mg as needed for nausea
- No known drug allergies
**Current Findings:**
- Temperature 37.0 °C (98.6 °F); pulse 88
- Tooth 30: large carious lesion into the pulp, nonrestorable; tender to percussion
- Fluctuant buccal swelling next to tooth 30; periapical radiolucency at both roots
- Blood counts drawn this morning: platelets 55,000/mm3; absolute neutrophil count 1,600/mm3

**Stem:** Following the AAPD best practices for patients receiving immunosuppressive therapy, which is the most appropriate plan for tooth 30?

A. Plan with his physician, then extract in a hospital with supportive measures; his platelets are below 60,000/mm3
B. Confirm with his oncologist, then extract in the office with local hemostatic measures; his platelets are above 50,000/mm3
C. Defer all dental treatment until chemotherapy ends; elective care waits during immunosuppression
D. Give a single dose of amoxicillin and extract today in the office; his neutrophils are below 2,000/mm3

**Keyed answer:** A

**Explanation:** Below 60,000 platelets/mm3, the AAPD best practices defer elective care and avoid invasive procedures when possible; when treatment is medically necessary, it belongs in a hospital setting after the dentist and physician agree on supportive measures such as platelet transfusion. An abscessed, nonrestorable tooth with swelling is a dental emergency, so it cannot wait for chemotherapy to end. His neutrophil count (1,600/mm3) falls in the AAPD band where the dentist uses clinical judgment (1,000-2,000/mm3). While the hospital visit is arranged, antibiotic coverage for the infection is decided with the medical team. The tempting wrong answer uses 50,000/mm3, a platelet floor many teaching sources give; the question names the AAPD guidance, which uses 60,000.

**Why each option:**
- A: Correct. Under 60,000/mm3, AAPD places medically necessary care in a hospital setting, after supportive measures such as platelet transfusion are planned with the physician.
- B: Talking to the oncologist is right, but 50,000/mm3 is a common teaching floor; the AAPD guidance named in the question treats counts under 60,000/mm3 as needing hospital-based care.
- C: An abscessed, swollen, nonrestorable tooth is an emergency, not elective care; AAPD has the physician help plan its treatment rather than waiting out chemotherapy.
- D: A neutrophil count of 1,000-2,000/mm3 calls for clinical judgment under AAPD; the central line is a question for the oncologist, not a reason for a routine single dose; and an antibiotic does nothing about his low platelets.

**Memory cue:** AAPD chemo counts: platelets under 60,000 = hospital + physician; ANC 1,000-2,000 = judgment; ANC under 1,000 = defer elective care.
