### R04

**Stem:** Pallor and nausea preceded a brief loss of consciousness during the injection, followed by rapid recovery. Which event best fits this transient episode?

A. Vasovagal syncope
B. Generalized seizure
C. Hypoglycemia
D. Stroke

**Keyed answer:** A

**Explanation:** Pain or anxiety around an injection can trigger vasovagal syncope, often preceded by pallor and nausea with rapid, complete recovery. The trigger and recovery pattern fit this transient episode.

**Why each option:**
- A: Correct. A trigger, pallor, nausea, and rapid spontaneous recovery fit vasovagal syncope.
- B: The prodrome and rapid recovery fit the vasovagal pattern better than the seizure choice.
- C: The box does not report a low glucose or a known glucose disorder.
- D: The brief trigger-linked event with rapid recovery is not the pattern asked about here.

**Memory cue:** Pallor, nausea, a trigger, and rapid recovery favor vasovagal syncope.

**Tags:** cc CC1; fk FK6; cognitive level 2; difficulty 2; phase dx

**Cited facts:**
- SYN-1 [verified]: Syncope is a transient loss of consciousness caused by transient global cerebral hypoperfusion, with rapid onset, short duration and spontaneous, complete recovery. Vasovagal syncope (the "common faint") is a reflex syncope set off by emotion or orthostatic stress; in the dental office pain, fear and anxiety trigger a reflex bradycardia and hypotension that reduce cerebral blood flow. It is usually preceded by prodromal symptoms of autonomic activation (sweating, pallor, nausea). (Conditions: Definitions quoted from the ESC 2009 guideline (open in PMC); it has been replaced by the 2018 ESC guideline (not openly fetchable), which keeps the same definition of syncope and reflex syncope.)
  - Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of syncope (version 2009) (1.1 Definitions): "Syncope is a T-LOC due to transient global cerebral hypoperfusion characterized by rapid onset, short duration, and spontaneous complete recovery."
  - Guidelines for the diagnosis and management of syncope (version 2009) (1.2.2.1 Reflex syncope): "also known as the ‘common faint’, is mediated by emotion or by orthostatic stress. It is usually preceded by prodromal symptoms of autonomic activation (sweating, pallor, nausea)."
  - Emergency drug kits: pharmacological and technical considerations (Drugs for hypotension (syncope)): "Pain, fear, and anxiety trigger a reflex leading to bradycardia and hypotension that reduces cerebral blood flow, leading to loss of consciousness."
- SYN-4 [verified]: Managing vasovagal syncope (P-C-A-B-D): at the first sign, place the patient supine with the legs level with or slightly above the head (a full Trendelenburg tilt of 15-30 degrees is controversial); the main danger while unconscious is airway obstruction from muscle relaxation, so check the carotid pulse, open the airway with a head-tilt, chin-lift and confirm breathing. Supine positioning with the legs raised usually brings recovery. (Conditions: Dental narrative review (Li and Ouanounou 2026, citing ADA/Malamed) and a CE review (Becker 2014). The general P-C-A-B-D frame: EM-4.)
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment): "The standard management approach for vasovagal syncope in dental settings follows the P–CAB–D protocol"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment: Positioning): "support placing the patient in a supine position with the legs level with or slightly elevated above the head at the earliest sign of syncope to improve venous return"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment: Positioning): "The Trendelenburg position, in which the feet are elevated 15–30° above the head, remains controversial"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Clinical phases: Syncope): "The greatest concern in syncope is airway obstruction caused by generalized muscular relaxation"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment: CAB): "A patent airway should be established using the head-tilt, chin-lift maneuver"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment: CAB): "Circulation is assessed by palpating the carotid pulse"
  - Syncope in dentistry: risk factors, preventive strategies, and management (Treatment: CAB): "breathing should be confirmed by observing chest rise, listening for breath sounds, and feeling airflow"
  - Emergency drug kits: pharmacological and technical considerations (Drugs for hypotension (syncope)): "Primary assessment and reclining the patient with legs elevated generally results in recovery."

  - First Aid for Seizures (What to avoid): "Don't offer water or food until they are fully alert."
  - First Aid for Seizures (Generalized seizures): "Turn them gently onto one side with their mouth pointing to the ground. This will keep their airway clear."
