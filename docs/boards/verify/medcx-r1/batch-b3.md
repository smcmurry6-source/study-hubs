**Current Findings:**

**Stem:** For an adult with significant cardiovascular disease, what total epinephrine amount is commonly used as the cautionary limit?

A. 0.02 mg
B. 0.04 mg
C. 0.10 mg
D. 0.20 mg

**Keyed answer:** B

**Explanation:** The ADA and cardiovascular teaching sources commonly use 0.04 mg as the reduced epinephrine limit for significant cardiovascular disease. The tempting wrong answer is 0.20 mg, the commonly cited healthy-adult maximum.

**Why each option:**
- A: This is below the commonly cited cardiovascular caution limit.
- B: Correct. A common recommendation for significant cardiovascular disease is to limit the total to 0.04 mg.
- C: This amount is above the cautionary limit.
- D: This is the commonly cited healthy-adult maximum, not the reduced cardiovascular limit.

**Memory cue:** Significant cardiovascular disease: keep total dental epinephrine near 0.04 mg.

**Tags:** cc CC8; fk FK8; cognitive level 2; difficulty 2; phase plan

**Cited facts:**
- VC-1 [verified]: Cardiac dose ceiling: the healthy-adult maximum of epinephrine in dental anesthesia is commonly given as 0.2 mg, lowered to 0.04 mg for patients with significant cardiovascular disease (ASA III-IV). 0.04 mg = about 2 cartridges of 1:100,000 (0.017-0.018 mg each; see LA-3, LA-6) or about 4 cartridges of 1:200,000. Keep it as "0.04 mg"; the cartridge count is a derived teaching number. (Conditions: 0.04 mg is about 2 cartridges of 1:100,000 (derived teaching number; VC-8 gives ADA's own conversion).)
  - Clinical assessment of the safe use local anaesthesia with vasoconstrictor agents in cardiovascular compromised patients: a systematic review (Background): "The maximum dose of epinephrine in local anaesthesia for a healthy subject is 0.2 mg, though this can be lowered to 0.04 mg if patient has severe cardiovascular disease (ASA III and IV)"
- VC-8 [verified]: ADA Oral Health Topic "Hypertension": when the history calls for caution, a common recommendation is to limit epinephrine to 0.04 mg in adults = 1 cartridge of 1:50,000, 2 of 1:100,000 or 4 of 1:200,000; epinephrine-impregnated retraction cord is discouraged in uncontrolled hypertension. This is the keyed number for the bank.
  - Hypertension (Oral Health Topics) (Local Anesthetics with Vasoconstrictors): "limit the epinephrine dose to 0.04 mg in adults"
  - Hypertension (Oral Health Topics) (Local Anesthetics with Vasoconstrictors): "cartridge of anesthetic containing 1:50,000 epinephrine; Two cartridges of anesthetic containing 1:100,000 epinephrine; or Four cartridges of anesthetic containing 1:200,000 epinephrine"
  - Hypertension (Oral Health Topics) (Local Anesthetics with Vasoconstrictors): "their use has been discouraged"
- BP-6 [verified]: Epinephrine limit in significant cardiovascular disease: 0.04 mg.
  - Hypertension (Oral Health Topics) (Local Anesthetics with Vasoconstrictors): "a common recommendation is to limit the epinephrine dose to 0.04 mg in adults"

### R02

**Stem:** Which follow-up question best helps judge whether elective dental care should proceed?

A. Has she ever used a spacer with fluticasone?
B. Does she prefer morning or afternoon appointments?
C. Ask about recent albuterol use and current wheezing
D. Would she like to stop all inhalers before the visit?

**Keyed answer:** C

**Explanation:** Rescue-inhaler frequency helps assess asthma control, and current wheezing or poor control is a reason to reschedule. The tempting wrong answer focuses on appointment preference rather than symptoms and rescue use.

**Why each option:**
- A: A spacer history does not replace assessing symptoms or rescue frequency.
- B: Appointment preference does not establish whether asthma is controlled.
- C: Correct. Rescue use more than two days a week generally signals poor control; current wheezing also matters.
- D: Stopping prescribed inhalers is not a way to assess control or prepare for dental care.

### R03

**Stem:** If she can use an inhaler during a future isolated bronchospasm without hives or airway swelling, which listed medicine is her rapid-acting rescue?

A. No rescue medicine; wait for symptoms to end
B. Inhaled albuterol
C. Inhaled fluticasone
D. Oral prednisone

**Keyed answer:** B

**Explanation:** Albuterol is a short-acting bronchodilator used for acute bronchospasm; fluticasone is a controller and does not provide immediate relief. The tempting wrong answer is the controller already listed in her history.

**Why each option:**
- A: Acute bronchospasm needs active rescue treatment; albuterol is the short-acting inhaled option.
- B: Correct. Albuterol is the short-acting inhaled rescue medicine.
- C: Fluticasone is a maintenance controller, not a rapid bronchodilator.
- D: Oral prednisone is not the listed short-acting rescue inhaler.

**Memory cue:** Fluticasone controls; albuterol relieves acute bronchospasm.

**Tags:** cc CC9; fk FK6; cognitive level 2; difficulty 2; phase tx

**Cited facts:**
- RES-7 [verified]: An inhaled corticosteroid (e.g., fluticasone) is a maintenance controller, not a bronchodilator: it does not relieve acute bronchospasm and is contraindicated as primary treatment of status asthmaticus or other acute episodes that need intensive measures. Acute asthma symptoms are treated with an inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonist such as albuterol, the most effective medication for relieving acute bronchospasm. (Conditions: Not quoted: since the 2020 NHLBI focused update (and in GINA), some patients use one budesonide-formoterol inhaler as both controller and reliever; a corticosteroid-only inhaler such as fluticasone is never the reliever. For an in-office attack, albuterol is the expected answer.)
  - Fluticasone propionate HFA inhalation aerosol (authorized generic of Flovent HFA) (1 Indications and usage): "Fluticasone Propionate HFA is not indicated for the relief of acute bronchospasm."
  - Fluticasone propionate HFA inhalation aerosol (authorized generic of Flovent HFA) (4 Contraindications): "Primary treatment of status asthmaticus or acute episodes of asthma requiring intensive measures."
  - Fluticasone propionate HFA inhalation aerosol (authorized generic of Flovent HFA) (17 Patient counseling information): "Advise patients to treat acute asthma symptoms with an inhaled, short-acting beta 2 -agonist such as albuterol."
  - Expert Panel Report 3: Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Asthma (Full Report 2007) (Section 3, Component 4: Safety of inhaled SABAs (key points)): "SABAs are the most effective medication for relieving acute bronchospasm (Evidence A)."

  - Recognition and management of complications during moderate and deep sedation part 1: respiratory considerations (Pulmonary aspiration): "management should follow that for an asthmatic attack; a bronchodilator should be administered, eg, albuterol 2–4 inhalations."

### R04

**Stem:** What is the first-line medication and route for this adult’s reaction?

A. Oral diphenhydramine while she remains seated
B. Epinephrine 0.3 mg IM in the thigh
C. Inhaled fluticasone followed by observation
D. Albuterol alone until the hives fade

**Keyed answer:** B

**Explanation:** Wheezing with hives, hoarseness, lip swelling, and hypotension points to anaphylaxis. Give adult-dose epinephrine intramuscularly in the thigh first; the tempting wrong answer is an oral antihistamine, which does not treat airway or circulatory findings.

**Why each option:**
- A: Oral diphenhydramine alone does not treat airway or circulatory findings.
- B: Correct. At 64 kg, the auto-injector dose is 0.3 mg IM in the anterolateral thigh.
- C: Fluticasone is not an acute rescue medicine and does not treat anaphylaxis.
- D: Albuterol alone does not address the systemic allergic reaction and hypotension.

**Memory cue:** Anaphylaxis: epinephrine IM in the thigh first.

**Tags:** cc CC1; fk FK8; cognitive level 2; difficulty 2; phase tx

**Cited facts:**
- EM-1 [verified]: Epinephrine auto-injector for anaphylaxis: 0.3 mg (EpiPen) for patients 30 kg (66 lb) or more; 0.15 mg (EpiPen Jr) for patients 15 to 30 kg (33 to 66 lb); injected intramuscularly or subcutaneously into the anterolateral thigh, through clothing if necessary. Each device is a single dose.
  - EpiPen and EpiPen Jr (epinephrine auto-injector), Viatris (2 Dosage and Administration): "Patients greater than or equal to 30 kg (66 lbs): EpiPen 0.3 mg"
  - EpiPen and EpiPen Jr (epinephrine auto-injector), Viatris (2 Dosage and Administration): "Patients 15 to 30 kg (33 lbs to 66 lbs): EpiPen Jr 0.15 mg"
  - EpiPen and EpiPen Jr (epinephrine auto-injector), Viatris (2 Dosage and Administration): "Inject intramuscularly or subcutaneously into the anterolateral aspect of the thigh, through clothing if necessary."
- RES-9 [verified]: Epinephrine in an asthma attack: if the patient cannot use the inhaler (too distressed or uncooperative), give epinephrine 0.3 mg IM (1:1,000 concentration) in an adult (0.15 mg in a child, the anaphylaxis dose). (Conditions: Dental CE reviews (Becker 2014; Becker and Haas 2011), not a guideline.   - Recognition and management of complications during moderate and deep sedation part 1: respiratory considerations (Pulmonary aspiration): "If the patient cannot cooperate with this form of drug administration, epinephrine 0.3 mg (a 1 ∶ 1000 concentration) should be administered intramuscularly."
