Daily drill new: 20 or 30

A set of questions a day per hub, fixed for the day: spaced-review questions that are due, questions you missed last time, then high-yield ones the class finds hard, spread across lectures. Each card says why it was picked. Pick the length before you start: 10, 20 or 30 questions (it remembers your choice). Finish for a results screen (the check draws itself, your score counts up, each question ticks in with a check or an X, and the drill streak rolls up when that set started today's streak), buttons for 10, 20 or 30 more, and a drill streak.

Rewards on every answer new

XP counts up on the rank pill in the bar, the goal ring fills, the day-streak flame grows with the streak, and Timmy cheers or flinches. When you leave the questions with 5 or more answered, a session recap card says how it went and offers the misses. It never interrupts practice.

Daily goal new

A ring in the bar fills toward your goal: 10 to 50 questions a day (20 to start; change it in Settings). The streak still counts any day you answer a question; missing the goal doesn't break it. On Home, the goal is a ring of 20 teeth that fill in as you answer.

Answer feedback new

Answer a question and a wash of green sweeps across the right choice with a soft glow and a check mark that draws itself. A wrong pick shakes and gets an X, then the right answer lights up. Select-all and ordering questions mark each option in turn, matching pairs light up as they lock in, and the explanation slides open. Works wherever you answer questions: the question bank, Weak Spots and the Daily drill.

Streak flame new

Get 3 right in a row and a flame appears in the bottom-right corner counting your run, with a ring that fills toward 100 (the tactical nuke). It grows and changes color as you go: orange at 5, red-hot at 10, blue at 25, violet at 50 and gold at 100. Miss one and it fizzles out in a puff of smoke. It counts the run on the page you're on, like the nuke.

Off with Settings → Extras → Streak flame, or with Focus mode. (The flame on the hub's bar is a different one: it counts the days in a row you've studied.) It steps aside on the Mock Exam tab.

Spaced review

Every question you get right comes back after 1, then 2, 4 and 7 days, never later than the day before the exam. Misses come back sooner. It feeds the drill and Weak Spots.

Studying now new shared

The green dot in the bar counts everyone on this hub right now, you included, and Home says it under the countdown. It's a live total: nothing about who.

Focus mode new

One switch at the top of Settings turns Timmy and every extra off, for just the notes and the questions. Turning it off brings back what you had, and turning any one extra back on ends it. It uses the extras' own switches, so the dashboard and every hub follow it.

Class accuracy on every question

After you answer, the explanation shows what % of the class got it right. Explanations name the most popular wrong answer when it's a common trap.

How the class answered new shared: once you answer a multiple-choice or select-all question, every choice shows the % of the class that picked it, with a light bar behind it, and the most common pick is in bold. A wrong answer with a big share is the trap most people fall for. Your own answer is counted in, and the line under the explanation says how many answers it's based on.

In the Question Bank, Weak Spots and the Daily drill in every hub, and on your mock exam's results (never while you're taking it). It shows once at least one other person has answered that question. Every answer counts, so someone who answers a question three times counts three times.

Why each choice new

Every multiple-choice and select-all question explains each answer choice, not just the right one. After you answer, tap the folded "Why each choice" bar under the explanation to see why the right answer is right and what is wrong with each of the others, with your pick marked. It starts folded so it doesn't fill the screen. It shows in the Question Bank, the Daily Drill and when you review a mock exam. Every new question gets one too.

Find this in the notes new

Miss a question and a Find this in the notes button appears under the explanation. Tap it to read the notes paragraph that matches the question, right there in the card, with its key words marked; Open this in the notes takes you to that spot in the lecture and highlights it. It works in the Question Bank, Weak Spots and the Daily drill (opening the notes closes the drill; it picks up where you left off), not in the mock exam or the arcade. When no paragraph matches well enough, the button doesn't show.

Good question? new

Under every explanation, once you answer, a thumbs up or down for the question itself. A thumbs down asks what's wrong with it: the answer looks wrong, the wording is confusing, it isn't in the notes, the explanation didn't help, or it gives the answer away (for anything else, report it opens the Report form). Tap the same thumb again to take your vote back.

Together with how the class does on each question (how many get it right the first time, who gets it right after missing it, how long it takes, whether people give up on it or skip the explanation), your votes put questions that may have a problem on a list that gets checked against the notes. Nothing changes a question on its own: a person reads it, and a fix goes out like any other.

In the Question Bank, Weak Spots, the Daily drill and your mock exam's results (never while you're taking it), in every hub. One vote per question; it's remembered on this device.

What the hubs record new

There's no sign-in and no email. Your browser gets a random id, kept on this device, that ties your progress, rank and trophies together (Link my devices joins two browsers). Under that id the hubs save: time in each part of a hub (only while the tab is in view and you've used it in the last 15 minutes), which buttons and tabs you tap, your answers (right or wrong, which choice, whether it was your first try, and how long you spent on the question and on its explanation; a question you read for a few seconds and leave unanswered is noted too), which features you use (Listen, Timmy Time, diagrams, the drill) and which settings you have on, and whatever you send (reports, suggestions, check-ins, surveys, comments, a thumbs up or down on a question). Words typed into Search are saved with no id at all. While you have a hub open, the dashboard's "studying now" count includes you, along with the outfit your Timmy has on, with no name or id; none of that is saved, and it's gone when you close the tab. Nothing else you type is recorded, no IP address, location or device details are saved with your data, and there are no ad or analytics trackers from other companies.

It's used to make the hubs better for the whole class: what gets used, which questions trip people up, what to fix before the next exam. Only Sam sees study data per person. Classmates see only what the hubs are built to show: leaderboards, trophies and comments under your leaderboard name (a generated dentistry name unless you pick one), and class-wide numbers such as the share of the class who got a question right. Nothing is used to grade or judge anyone. Clearing this site's data in your browser starts a new id, and your progress on that device goes with it.

How long it's kept new: time in each part of a hub is saved in 25-second steps for 30 days, then only as hourly totals. Which buttons and tabs you tapped is kept for 90 days, and which features you used for 180 days; after that only class-wide daily totals remain, with no id. A report or suggestion loses your id 120 days after Sam replies (what it said and the reply stay), a survey answer 90 days after the survey closes, and an exam check-in after a year. Your answers, XP, rank and trophies are kept, because your progress is built from them. The cleanup runs by itself once a week.

new With the redesign: your chats with Ask Timmy are saved, every message with your browser's id, to help improve the hub (Sam reads them on the admin page to fix gaps in the notes and wrong answers). They're answered by Claude, Anthropic's AI: your question, the hub's notes and what's on your screen go to Anthropic's API to write the answer, and nowhere else. Only Sam and your own browser can read your chats; classmates never see them.

An idea you post on the idea board shows to the class under your leaderboard name, and so does a check-in's "Anything the hub could do better?" unless you untick the box. Your votes and reports on ideas are kept so each person counts once; nobody else sees who voted or reported.

How you rated each flashcard and where you are in Learn are kept, so your schedule and progress follow you to your other devices. What you bought in Timmy's shop and what's out at his place are kept, so they stay yours on every device.

Counted, with nothing about who or which: opening Timmy's place and changing something there (which spot, never which piece); opening the shop, a purchase (its kind: an outfit, a snack, a boost, never which piece) and a rare costume turning up; a visit to someone's place (that one happened, never whose); a bowling clip playing (which clip, never who saw it). The clips you've seen and your run of right answers are kept on your device only.

The outfit your Timmy has on goes with the studying-now count, with no name or id, and isn't saved.

Inside a hub new look

Each hub has four parts on its bar: Home, Notes, Questions and More. On a laptop the bar is at the top; on a phone it's at the bottom, with a Settings button beside the four. Your answers, pins, highlights and progress in each hub carried over from the old pages.

Back to the dashboard new

Always on the screen in a hub: All hubs at the start of the bar on a laptop, Hubs at the start of the top strip on a phone. (More → All hubs is still there.)

Pages and pop-ups move new

A hub's pages slide in from the side their tab is on, the tab you tapped pops, a hub's panel on the dashboard slides in the same way, and every pop-up window (the shop, Timmy's place, the trophy case, the Inbox, a check-in, a survey) rises into place. Off with your device's reduced-motion setting or Settings → Answer animations.

Home new

Every hub opens on its own Home: the hub's masthead, the exam card (a live countdown and the announced facts), quick links on the first phone screen (practice questions, Daily drill, the notes you were on, the mock, and Timmy Time when the hub has an episode), Where to next (at most three: yesterday's misses in exam week, your weakest lecture, a lecture not started), Today (the goal as a ring of 20 teeth, your streak, XP today, right today, Timmy's HP), progress by lecture with Notes and Questions buttons, and the course timeline. Under the countdown: how many are studying this hub right now.

Notes new

The lecture list (lectures on the exam first, the others for review, each with a progress bar and how many questions you've tried), three reading levels (As taught, Plain English, High yield), Listen with Follow along, the drawn diagrams beside their paragraph, each lecture's charts under its notes, and Check yourself: three questions at the end of each lecture's notes. At the end, Previous lecture / Next lecture, and Report a mistake in these notes (also in the notes toolbar), which opens the report form with the lecture attached.

Questions new

The Question Bank, its filter panel (with the High likelihood toggle) and four sets: Daily drill, Yesterday's misses, Weak spots and Mock exam. Answer choices are plain rows with a check or a cross after you answer; Pin and Report are on every card. After an answer, one row: Try it again, Find this in the notes (on a miss), Ask Timmy and Good question?.

Study as: Questions, Learn or Flashcards new

At the top of Questions. The panel picks which questions; this picks how. Learn and Flashcards show a card with how far along you are and a Start button, then take the whole screen, one card at a time, like Quizlet: a Close button (or Escape) puts you back in the bank, and your progress is kept.

Learn asks the questions you picked in rounds of 7 until you've got each right twice (the second time, when the question allows it, you think of the answer before the choices show; a miss comes back at the end of the round). The choices are big tiles (keys 1 to 6 pick one on a laptop), Don't know? shows the answer without a guess, and after a right answer it moves on by itself unless you touch the card to read why (the Auto-next switch turns that off). A bar along the top shows how far along you are, and each round ends with what you got.

Flashcards show the question on a card; you flip it (Flip the card, a tap on the card or the space bar) and say how well you knew it: Again, Hard, Good or Easy, keys 1 to 4, the left and right arrows for Again and Good, or on a phone a swipe left for Again and right for Good. It comes back when you're about to forget it, never later than the day before the exam. When you're caught up you see how the session went, rating by rating. Home tells you when cards are due.

Learn's answers count like any answer. A flashcard counts toward your daily goal and streak (not as an answer: no class stats, no "Last time") and earns 2 XP when it's due or new, for its first three ratings a day; Study ahead earns none. Drill, Yesterday's misses and Weak spots work in all three; the mock exam is always questions.

Question types

Multiple choice, select-all with partial credit, matching, ordering, two-statement, EXCEPT / NOT, Patient Box cases and picture questions, each in the exam's own format.

Which statement is true, or false? new: four full statements, and you pick the one true statement (or, in the inverse, the one false statement among three true ones). Each false statement changes one detail you were taught, never a trick of wording, and every choice explains itself once you answer. Find them under the Question Bank's More filters → question type (Heme & Endo Exam 2 and Occlusion's final lectures for now; every new lecture gets them).

Patient pictures new: some Patient Box cases in Heme & Endo now show an illustration of the findings the box lists (the gums in Addison's disease, the face and hands in acromegaly, a patient on long-term prednisone). They're painted for the hub, not photos of real patients, and they only show what the box already says, so they never give the answer away.

Patient cases drawn from their numbers new: in the Perio and PCD Implants hubs, Patient Box cases now come with a periodontal chart, a periapical radiograph and a drawn view of the gums, all produced from the same case numbers (probing depths, recession, bleeding, mobility, furcations, bone levels, restorations, implants), the way the Perio midterm check-ins asked. Every tooth is drawn from its real anatomy (the sizes and shapes in Wheeler's Dental Anatomy: the right cusps, the right number of roots, the long canine root, the root trunk above a molar's furcation), and every number in a case is checked to be realistic and to agree with the others before it ships. The chart is drawn like a probing chart (blue line for the gingival margin, red line for the pocket base, red dots for bleeding, furcation triangles), with the numbers in a table under it. Everything you need to answer is in the box before you answer, and a picture never shows the answer: a question that asks you to work out the attachment level hides the CAL row, one that asks the furcation class hides the furcation row. After you answer, "Why each choice" explains every option and In the notes buttons jump to the notes paragraph and Review table the case comes from. The drawings are illustrations made from the numbers, never patient photos or real radiographs.
